# Design decisions

This is the "why did we do that?" document. Each entry records a non-obvious choice, the alternatives considered, and the tradeoff we accepted. New entries should follow the same shape.

Not an exhaustive list of every tweak — just the decisions where someone reading the code might reasonably wonder "why this way and not another?"

---

## Simulation biology

### Energy pyramid — predators get 10% of prey's stored energy
**Chosen:** when a predator kills prey, it is offered `prey_energy × 0.1` (`PREDATION_TRANSFER_FRACTION`) and keeps that times its digestion efficiency for the victim's tissue: `Genome::animal_efficiency()` for an animal, `plant_efficiency()` for a plant (animal efficiency since 2026-09-19, tissue-based since 2026-09-23, see "Grazing through eat"; the undigested part of the offer is booked to the ledger's `digestion` flow, the rest of the prey's energy to `death`).
**Alternatives:** full energy transfer (100%), half (50%), fixed constant.
**Why:** thermodynamically honest — most energy is lost as heat in real ecosystems. Also functions as a balance mechanism: predators can't sustain themselves indefinitely on abundant prey, preventing predator-dominated attractors.
**Accepted tradeoff:** predators need dense prey to thrive; in sparse populations they struggle. This is realistic but can mean predator lineages fail on some seeds.

### One kill per victim per tick — first attacker wins
**Chosen:** `predation_system` tracks the victims claimed during the current tick. The first attacker to land a kill on a target takes the 10% energy transfer; later attackers skip that target and carry on scanning for another one.
**Alternatives:** split the transfer between every attacker that picked the victim; pay every attacker in full (the previous behaviour, by omission).
**Why:** several attackers can pick the same victim in one tick. Before this rule each of them was paid 10% of the victim's unmodified energy and each pushed a kill, so one death was paid several times over and `PredationStats.kills` exceeded predation deaths (4724 kills against 1946 predation deaths at the baseline seed of the 2026-09-17 review). Splitting the transfer would also conserve energy, but the payout would then depend on how many neighbours happened to fire in the same tick, which is noise rather than a selective signal. First-wins keeps the payout a fixed fraction of the victim's energy, and the attacker that loses the race is free to find another target in the same tick.
**Accepted tradeoff:** which attacker wins is query iteration order, not a contest of size or speed. At one tick of resolution this is indistinguishable from simultaneity, and the loser pays nothing for the attempt.

### Attackers strike the nearest passing target
**Chosen (2026-09-20):** `predation_system` gates every neighbour the spatial hash returns within attack range and strikes the nearest one that passes (`nearest_target`), rather than the first one in the list. The one-claim-per-victim rule above, the gates, and the graze/kill split are unchanged.
**Alternatives:** keep first-in-list (the previous behaviour); prefer plants for grazers and animals for hunters by diet (rejected here: that decides what the `attack` output means, which is the open design question of separate graze and attack outputs, and the sim should not programme the preference); strike every passing target (rejected: it multiplies the payout by neighbour density).
**Why:** `query_radius` returns neighbours in hash bucket order, so the first passing neighbour was an arbitrary one. A grazer moving towards a plant had a consumer chosen for it whenever the consumer sorted earlier, and killed it for almost nothing at its animal efficiency. In the 2026-09-20 phase 1 audit no hunters were alive, yet predation deaths equalled kills at 24k to 73k per 15k-tick run. Nearest is the physical reading (you bite what is in front of you) and costs one short scan per attacker instead of a `break`.

The change stands on its own terms, because the previous choice was an artefact and this one is the physical reading, but it does not settle the bystander-kill problem; that still points at the design question of separate graze and attack outputs, and at `hunter-emergence`.

**Accepted tradeoff:** the funnel stats `targets_considered`, `rejected_size_gate` and `rejected_damage` now count every in-range neighbour rather than the ones up to the first hit, so their absolute values rise against earlier audits while the strike count does not. Ties in distance still fall to list order.

### Child starting energy is a fraction of what the parent paid
**Chosen:** a child is born with `CHILD_ENERGY_FRACTION` (0.8) of the parent's actual reproduction cost, which is `reproduction_energy_cost × (0.5 + body_size × 0.5)`. At body size 1.0 that is 32 energy, the value every child received before; at the 0.3 floor it is 20.8.
**Alternatives:** a fixed child energy regardless of parent size (the previous behaviour); the child receives the full cost with no overhead; neither cost nor child energy scaled with size.
**Why:** the parent's cost scaled with body size but the child's energy was a fixed `reproduction_energy_cost × 0.8` = 32. Below body size 1.0 the parent paid less than the child received, so every birth created energy from nothing (2 at size 0.5, 6 at the 0.3 floor); above 1.0 the sign flipped and reproduction taxed large bodies. That is a gradient toward small bodies that no decision had chosen, and it sat underneath every observation about body-size drift elsewhere in this document. Tying the child to the parent's actual payment makes each birth conserve or lose energy at every size.
**Accepted tradeoff:** small-bodied children start with less energy than before and have less runway to their first meal, so small lineages lose a subsidy they had been evolving under. Watch the body-size average after this change; the 2026-09-17 review baseline sat at 0.81 for `--headless 1000 --seed 42`.

### Emergent carrying capacity — the cap stays at 2000, and it now announces itself
**Update 2026-09-20:** raised to 6000, the safety ceiling the design doc sized, in the same pull request as canopy light sharing. With the canopy, the uncapped seed 42 world no longer runs to the ceiling: plants oscillate between 1400 and 4300 and grazers between 1000 and 2500, so the population settles in the low thousands on its own. Under the old 2000 the cap sat below that natural size and its replacement lottery reasserted plant dominance on every seed (1870 plants and 67 to 128 grazers at 5000 ticks on seeds 1, 3 and 42). Under 6000, seeds 1 and 42 held 1200 and 1900 grazers against 1400 and 750 plants at 5000 ticks with the ceiling touched once and released; seed 3, whose grazers never took hold in the opening, ran to 5895 plants and held the ceiling, so it is still a rule some seeds meet, now visibly. Tick cost at 2500 to 6000 organisms is two to three times the 2000-cap cost (contended, 475 to 720 s for 5000 ticks against 260 to 340 s). The rest of this entry is the 2026-09-18 state.
**Chosen:** `SimConfig::population_ceiling` (2000) is the only birth limiter in `reproduction_system`; the hard-coded `max_pop = 2000` is gone. Its only effect is to refuse births while the population is at or above it, and blocked parents keep their energy. Each time it engages the system writes one chronicle entry and increments `SimStats::ceiling_episodes`; the episode ends, with a second entry, once the population has fallen below `CEILING_RELEASE_FRACTION` (95%) of the ceiling, so a population hovering just under it does not reopen an episode every few ticks. Blocked births accumulate in `SimStats::ceiling_blocked_births`, and the headless summary prints the ceiling, the episode count, and the blocked-birth count. The value is 2000 rather than the 6000 the design asked for, for the reason below.
**Alternatives:** the 6000 safety ceiling from `docs/design/simulation-rules.md` ("Carrying capacity"); the previous silent cap; a cap per region or biome; no ceiling at all.
**Why:** every one of the 16 runs in the 2026-09-18 attractor audit ended at exactly 2000 organisms, so the design asked for the cap to become a high ceiling that only a runaway would touch, with the population plateauing where energy puts it. Measured, the plateau does not exist under the current rules. With the ceiling at 6000, every seed ran straight through 2000 and hit 6000 within 630 to 780 ticks, then pinned there with plants at 5700 to 6000 of 6000; a 15k run on seed 42 held the ceiling from tick 1080 to the end with 6000 plants and no foragers or predators. A plant alone on its tile has all the light it needs and nothing consumes it, so the population grows until a rule stops it; the cap is the carrying capacity. Shipping the ceiling at 2000 keeps today's behaviour, and shipping it instrumented turns the hidden rule into a visible one: every audit summary now says how often the cap engaged and how many births it blocked. A per-region cap would invent a boundary the sim does not otherwise have; no ceiling at all would run the machine into the ground with nothing to say why.

Runs with the ceiling at 6000, `--headless 5000 --seed S --dump-history`, before (main, cap 2000) and after:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2000 | 6000 | 106s | 358s | one episode from tick ~630 |
| 3 | 1781 | 6000 | 170s | 422s | one episode from tick ~780 |
| 42 | 2000 | 6000 | 247s | 1362s (shared machine) | two episodes: tick ~660, released at ~840 on a dip to ~4500, pinned from ~1080 |

The 6000 runs cost 2.5x to 3.4x the tick time on seeds 1 and 3, which ran on a lightly loaded machine; seed 42 overlapped a 15k run and other builds, so its time is an upper bound. In-process time is quoted throughout.
**Accepted tradeoff:** the design's done-when for this piece, a 15k-tick run that never touches the ceiling with the population plateauing where the ledger's flows put it, is not met, and cannot be met until phase 1 gives plants a consumer. Once the diet axis is in, the ceiling is raised and the plateau measured again. The first cost that raise will hit is inside `reproduction_system` itself: `already_mated.contains` and `mate_candidates.iter().find` are linear scans inside the per-organism loop, so reproduction cost grows roughly with the square of the population. The `TODO.md` items `spatial-hash-organisms-only`, `photosynthesis-density-cache`, and `rayon-remaining-systems` are the rest of the bill (see the design doc, "Performance is on the critical path").

### Quadratic costs for body, armor, claws, speed
**Chosen:** metabolism cost scales as `trait²` for these four traits.
**Alternatives:** linear costs, tiered cliffs, no extra cost.
**Why:** prevents "stack everything" meta. If costs were linear, evolution would converge on maxed-out big armored fast predators. Quadratic costs force trade-offs — you can be fast *or* armored *or* big, but not all three cheaply.
**Accepted tradeoff:** organisms might never evolve extreme traits because the marginal cost becomes prohibitive. If we see everyone converging to tiny low-trait organisms, that's the sign we've overtuned it.

### Photosynthetic surface drag — `speed × 1 / (1 + photo_area × photo_drag)`
**Chosen (2026-09-20, step 2 of `plans/2026-09-20-plant-physics.md`):** an organism's speed is multiplied by `1 / (1 + total photo surface area × photo_drag)`, beside the existing armour drag `1 / (1 + armour × 0.3)`. `SimConfig::photo_drag` is 1.0 by default (`--photo-drag`). A plant with the typical 1.5 to 1.7 units of surface moves at 37% to 40% of the speed it had; a photosynthesiser with a single small surface is barely touched.
**Alternatives:** a rule that photosynthesisers cannot move (rejected: it names a strategy, and nothing in nature says it; algae swim); coupling drag to `photosynthesis_rate` (rejected: the rate is chemistry, the surface is the thing with physical consequences, and a high-rate small-surface organism is a plausible motile alga); a movement energy surcharge instead of a speed cut (not taken: cost is already proportional to distance, so slower already means cheaper per tick, and a plant's surplus made any energy price invisible until light is scarce).
**Why:** what roots an oak is that light is a thin income, so a light-eater spreads a large flat surface, and a large flat surface is a sail. The sim already said this about armour plates; leaving leaves out of the same formula was the arbitrary choice. Step 1's instruments showed plants moving at nearly half an eater's speed (about 1.0 against 2.2 world units per tick) and paying for it from income the clamp discarded. Drag on its own is not expected to change the population outcome; it makes locomotion a thing a plant's surplus is spent on, so that when light becomes scarce (step 3, canopy sharing) sitting still is selected for rather than decreed. Carnivorous plants remain possible and become ambushers, which is what they are.

Grazer counts rise a little with drag (a slower plant is easier to bite and does not walk away from its grazer), and leaf area keeps rising under selection at every coefficient, which says drag costs a plant nothing it was using. 1.0 is kept: it is the physically moderate value (a sail drags more than a plate, three times armour's coefficient), and the plan makes no population claim for drag alone; that is step 3's job.

### Canopy light sharing — `light share = min(1, window tiles × capacity / leaf area in window)`
**Chosen (2026-09-20, step 3 of `plans/2026-09-20-plant-physics.md`):** each tick `photosynthesis_system` sums leaf area (`total_photo_surface_area`) per tile for every organism that earns any sun, builds a summed-area table, and gives each photosynthesiser the light share of its 9 by 9 tile window (`CANOPY_RADIUS` 4): the window's tiles times `SimConfig::leaf_capacity_per_tile` divided by the leaf area in the window, capped at 1. Where leaves exceed what the ground can light, everyone there is shaded in proportion; a lone plant with typical leaves is fully lit. This replaces the per-tile density penalty below. Capacity is the constant that sets how many fully lit plants the world holds (`--leaf-capacity`); at 0.02 a window fully lights 1.62 units of leaf, about one typical plant, so the world has room for roughly 3200 fully lit plants and shades everything beyond that in proportion.
**Alternatives:** the per-tile penalty kept and steepened (rejected three times over, see below: on 262k tiles it never engages); a radius query on the spatial hash per plant (rejected: it indexes food too and costs a filtered lookup per neighbour, against one pass over the grid here); nutrients consumed by growth (deferred to phase 2, where it couples to biomes); leaf upkeep by area (held in reserve, see the plan's open questions).
**Why:** step 3 of the diet-axis plan found that nothing bounds plant growth. A saturated plant earns about twelve times its upkeep, is always ready to breed, and doubles every 50 ticks when nothing stops it; under a ceiling the replacement lottery drifts to the permanently ready. Light per unit of ground is the finite thing the world already has, and sharing it over a canopy is the mechanism the design doc's "emergent from energy" carrying capacity was missing. The point is not the plant count on its own: a shaded plant earns near its upkeep and is not ready to breed, which removes the lottery advantage and the doubling, and it makes a plant's surplus scarce enough that spending it on locomotion (now dragged by the same leaves) has a price.
**Accepted tradeoffs:** the world wraps but the window clips at the map edge, so edge plants see fewer neighbours and are slightly favoured; the window is square, not a disc; the table is rebuilt every tick at 262k cells, which is cheap next to the neighbour queries the tick already runs.

### Plant density competition — `yield × 1/(1 + others_on_tile × 0.3)` (superseded 2026-09-20)
**Superseded** by canopy light sharing above. Kept for the tuning history.
**Chosen:** photosynthesis yield drops inversely with the number of other plants on the same tile.
**Alternatives:** no competition (100% plant worlds), linear penalty (too harsh — nobody survives clustering), carrying-capacity cliff (abrupt and unfair).
**Why:** biologically honest (real plants shade each other) and naturally caps monocultures without banning clustering.
**Accepted tradeoff:** on its own, this mechanism **doesn't** cap plant dominance. Swept the coefficient 0.2 → 0.5 → 2.0 in a 512² world with ~2000 organisms and plants still reached 90%+. The world is large enough that plants naturally spread to ~1 per tile, so density rarely bites. Density competition is real pressure *when plants cluster*, but it's not the thing that prevents monoculture. That's what `PHOTO_OUTPUT_MULTIPLIER` is for (separate entry).

### Photosynthesis output multiplier — raw scalar on yield
**Chosen:** a global `PHOTO_OUTPUT_MULTIPLIER` multiplier on the photosynthesis energy equation. 0.5 from the original tuning; 1.0 since 2026-09-17 (see the revision at the end of this entry).
**Alternatives:** steeper density penalty (see above — doesn't help), more aggressive metabolism costs for photosynthesisers (biased against a strategy rather than rebalancing), reducing sunlight (same effect, worse name).
**Why:** density competition on its own wasn't enough because plants don't cluster densely in a large world. The blunt lever is lowering the raw photosynthesis yield so plant energy intake becomes comparable to what foraging yields — at which point foragers can actually compete. Tuning journey: 2.0 (original) → 1.0 → 0.7 all produced 90%+ plant monocultures. 0.5 consistently produces diverse ecologies across four tested seeds (plant share ranges 38%–79%, foragers 7%–61%, predators 0.5%–14%).
**Accepted tradeoff:** at first read the death breakdown looked starvation-dominant (~85% of deaths). That turned out to be a misattribution bug in `metabolism_system` (see "Health regen gate" below) — once fixed, the split is more like ~30% starvation, ~50–80% predation, rest disease. **Revised framing: predation is the primary selection pressure, not starvation.** In a live run after the fix, one second showed Predation 84% / Disease 8% / Starvation 7% / Old age 1%. Point stands: foragers earn their place because photosynthesis is no longer a free lunch — they just earn it by being predated less than alternatives, not by out-eating the shortfall. Also notable: only ~7 organisms are genome-classified `SpeciesStrategy::Predator` (`claw_power > 0.5`), yet hundreds of kills per second happen — most predation is done by foragers with modest claws. The strategy label is decorative for display, not load-bearing for dynamics.

**Revised 2026-09-17, back to 1.0:** every value in the tuning journey above was measured while a killed photosynthesiser kept photosynthesising and could be killed and paid for again (see "Kills are explicit" below). Plants were never actually losing organisms to predation, so the multiplier was being lowered against a plant strategy that could not lose. With kills final, 0.5 sent plants extinct by 5000 ticks on seed 3 and left 29 on seed 42. Sweep at 5000 ticks over seeds 1, 2, 3 and 42: 0.75 kept plants alive everywhere with predators at 3, 5, 32 and 10; 1.0 kept plants alive everywhere with predators at 21, 1, 28 and 49. Seed 42 at 1.0 was then run five times: plants 1165 to 1468, foragers 530 to 825, predators 2 to 49, and at 0.5 three times: plants 3 to 29. Same-seed runs were not reproducible at the time (headless frame pacing followed the wall clock; see "Headless runs are deterministic" below), so the plant-side conclusion is robust across that spread and the predator counts are not a tuning signal at all. 1.0 chosen. The predator energy share (0.1) was also swept at 1.0: 0.15 and 0.2 did not sustain predators but flipped seeds between plant monoculture and plant extinction (seed 42 at 0.15: 0 plants, 809 predators; seed 3 at 0.2: 0 plants, 610 predators), so it stays at 0.1. Seeds 1 and 2 still drift to plant monoculture at 1.0 and predators decline slowly on every seed; the outcomes are bistable rather than tunable, which is a question for the simulation-rules design doc (`plans/2026-09-17-simulation-rules-rethink.md`), not for this constant.
### Kills are explicit — a `Killed(cause)` marker, and a corpse earns nothing
**Chosen:** `predation_system` inserts `Killed(DeathCause::Predation)` on each victim as well as zeroing its energy and health. `photosynthesis_system` and `symbiosis_transfer_system` filter on `Without<Killed>`. `death_system` despawns any organism with energy ≤ 0, health ≤ 0, or a `Killed` marker, and takes the cause from the marker when there is one; otherwise it attributes by old age, then disease, then starvation. `predation_system` also skips targets already at zero health.
**Alternatives:** gate photosynthesis and symbiosis on `health > 0` and keep inferring cause from health (smaller change, but leaves old-age deaths, which also reach zero health, counted as predation); despawn victims inside `predation_system` (loses the death marker and the per-cause bookkeeping in one place); reorder `death_system` to run directly after `predation_system` (still lets a corpse be paid for by symbiosis and photosynthesis on the next tick if anything revived it).
**Why:** `death_system` only despawned on energy ≤ 0. A killed photosynthesiser had its energy refilled by `photosynthesis_system` in the same tick, survived at zero health, could never regenerate because of the regen gate below, and was killed and paid for again on later ticks. In the 2026-09-17 accounting branch a diagnostic counted 1297 of 3039 kills landing on targets already at zero health, 1959 of them photosynthesisers; kills exceeded predation deaths on every run (4238 against 2476 at `--headless 1000 --seed 42` after the one-kill-per-victim rule). Predators were being fed from corpses, and the plant-dominated balance depended on it. Recording the cause at the kill also fixes old-age attribution: the "by Old age" line had always read 0 because health reaching zero was read as predation.
**Accepted tradeoff:** one more component and one more thing for a future system between `predation_system` and `death_system` to remember to filter on. Kills and predation deaths are now equal by construction (3389 and 3389 at the seed above). Removing the corpse income moves the ecology a long way; see the 2026-09-17 revision of the photosynthesis output multiplier entry above.

### Health regen gate — "a corpse doesn't heal"
**Chosen:** `metabolism_system`'s health regen step (`health += 0.005`) is gated on `health > 0.0`. Organisms whose health has been set to zero don't regen.
**Alternatives:** reorder systems so `death_system` runs before `metabolism_system`, make `predation_system` despawn victims directly, introduce a `Dying(cause)` marker component, use a large-negative sentinel for health that regen can't overcome.
**Why:** when the photosynthesis multiplier tuning shipped, death-cause attribution looked broken — 0 predation deaths across every seed despite thousands of attack events. Root cause: `predation_system` sets victim `health = 0` and `energy = 0`, then `metabolism_system` runs in the same tick and regens health to ~0.005 (because the `min(1.0)` clamp didn't have a corresponding `max(0.0)` floor for the dead), then `death_system` reads the victim as energy-dead but health-alive and classifies them as Starvation. Gating the regen on `health > 0.0` is the minimal invariant-preserving fix and reads naturally — a fatally wounded organism shouldn't regenerate between ticks. It also keeps the `death_system` cause-priority chain simple (health ≤ 0 → Predation) without introducing new components or ordering constraints.
**Accepted tradeoff:** a one-liner behavioural rule buried in `metabolism_system` that a future reader might not connect to attribution correctness. Mitigated by a comment at the gate explaining the predation-attribution coupling. Since the `Killed` marker (entry above) the gate is no longer what makes attribution correct; it stays as a guard so a zero-health organism never heals in the tick it dies.

### Energy ledger — per-flow accumulators, a per-tick residual, and a per-organism scratch record for the parallel systems
**Chosen:** `EnergyLedger` (in `clauvolution_core`) carries one accumulator per flow that moves organism energy: photosynthesis, food, predation (the killer's share), symbiosis (gross transfer), metabolism, movement, disease drain, reproduction paid by parents, reproduction received by children, energy lost at death, and energy destroyed at the `max_organism_energy` clamp. Every flow is a magnitude except death, which is the signed energy an organism held when removed and can be slightly negative when metabolism overdrew it on its final tick. Serial systems add to `ledger.tick` at the point they move energy. `photosynthesis_system` and `metabolism_system` run under `par_iter_mut` and cannot touch the resource, so each organism carries an `EnergyFlows` component (the same struct as the totals) that the owning iteration writes; `ledger_system`, placed after `reproduction_system` and before the recorders, sums those records serially, zeroes them, sums live energy, and books the residual: the change in total since the previous tick minus `tick.net()`. `death_system` and the asteroid/volcano path fold a dying organism's record and remaining energy into the ledger before the despawn removes them. Spawning and loading return the spawned energy and reset the baseline so tick 1 compares against a real total. Every `.min(max_organism_energy)` on income goes through `credit_clamped`, which returns what the cap discarded.
**Alternatives:** a debug assertion alone (no flows to read when it fires, and nothing in release); a `Mutex` or atomic accumulators inside the parallel closures (contention on the hot path, and against the invariant that parallel work touches no shared mutable state); recording the rounded f32 deltas actually applied to `Energy` instead of the amounts the rules computed (the residual would be exactly zero by construction at every instrumented site, and the flows would carry rounding noise instead of what the rules moved); running `ledger_system` at the very end of the chain after the recorders (the population snapshot would then see the previous tick's books).
**Why:** the three energy-minting bugs fixed on 2026-09-17 (multiple attackers paid for one victim, corpses photosynthesising and being killed again, children born with more than the parent paid) survived every tuning pass because no instrument could see energy being created. The residual is the detector: a flow that is missed, double-counted, or minted unrecorded shows up the same tick. The flows are the tuning instrument the design doc asks for, so phase 1 and 2 changes can be read as where the energy goes rather than only as population counts. The tolerance is 0.1 per tick: `Energy` is f32, so each write rounds by up to half an ulp (about 4e-6 at the 120 cap); with 2000 organisms and three to four writes each per tick the fully aligned worst case is around 0.03 and the typical value is under 1e-3, while the smallest rule-sized amount is one organism's 0.08 base metabolism. Wiring the flows found one more leak: `symbiosis_transfer_system` clamped both partners at zero after the transfer, so a payer near zero lost less than the receiver gained. The payer now gives only what it holds; its fate is unchanged (zero energy dies in `death_system` either way) and the receiver gets what was actually available.
**Accepted tradeoff:** 88 bytes of `EnergyFlows` per organism and one more component in the spawn bundle (three hand-synchronised copies, see `organism-spawn-bundle-triplicated`); an organism spawned without it drops out of the ledger's live total and shows up as a residual, which is the failure mode we want. Every new system that moves energy must book its flow or the residual fires; that is the point. The residual only sees energy the rules did not account for: a rule that legitimately books a wrong amount (a corpse being paid for) shows in the flows, not the residual. Measured at `--headless 5000` on seeds 42, 1 and 3: largest per-tick residual 0.00057, 0.00067 and 0.00067, no tick over tolerance, and the cumulative residual over the whole run 0.031, -0.086 and 0.059 against a live total near 225,000. The flows also put a number on something the population counts never showed: the clamp destroyed 7.4M, 6.1M and 6.3M energy over those runs, roughly as much as food delivered, which is a tuning question for the design doc's phases rather than for this instrument.

### Disease: direct mortality + energy drain, not drain alone
**Chosen:** infected organisms suffer both a per-tick energy drain AND a small per-tick chance of direct death. Resistance protection is quadratic — `drain × (1 - res × 0.5)²` and `mortality × (1 - res)²`.
**Alternatives:** drain only (first implementation), direct mortality only, linear (1-res) protection (v2 pass, superseded).
**Why direct mortality:** drain-only failed on photosynthesisers. Plants refill energy from sunlight faster than disease drained it, so they were immune in practice. Adding direct mortality that ignores energy reserves ensures disease can't be "sun-bathed" through.
**Why quadratic resistance:** at linear, resistance of 10% gave 90% mortality factor vs 100% for res=0 — nearly no fitness delta across the observed evolved range (5-20%), so selection drifted. Squared amplifies the delta at the top of the range (res=50% goes from 50% factor to 25%) and leaves the bottom mostly unchanged. Validated at 15k ticks across seeds 1, 7, 42, 99: infected population roughly halved on 3 of 4 seeds (359/276/266/359 vs linear 458/638/267/378), resistance +1-3 percentage points.
**Accepted tradeoff:** Disease remains a background mortality pressure (~5-10% of deaths), not a primary evolutionary driver. Predation dominates the fitness landscape at 55-75% of deaths, so resistance is always a second-order concern; even with the quadratic bump, resistance doesn't climb to the range where squared really pays off (40%+). Shipping because (a) infected populations dropping is a monotone improvement, (b) the math now correctly rewards high resistance when it does appear. The direct-mortality path still only zeroes `energy` (not `health`) so `death_system` correctly attributes the death to Disease rather than Predation.

### Symbiosis tuning: loose threshold + modest transfer
**Chosen:** `SYMBIOSIS_RANGE = 6.0`, `SYMBIOSIS_LINK_THRESHOLD = 10` ticks, `SYMBIOSIS_TRANSFER_RATE = 0.15` energy per tick at |rate|=1.0.
**Why this set of numbers (observed, not theorised):** v1 shipped with range 6, threshold 30, transfer 0.05 and produced 3–35 pairs per 6000-tick seed — a barely-present dynamic. Widening range to 12 actually produced FEWER pairs (3–4) because more nearby candidates made the "nearest stays nearest" streak flaky. Shortening the streak to 10 ticks was the real unlock — pair count jumped to 66–124 per seed, so the mechanic now contributes something visible every tick rather than a handful of links per run. Transfer was tripled to 0.15 to give any individual link a measurable fitness impact; doubling further to 0.30 produced no extra selection signal and was reverted.
**Open question on selection:** avg evolved `symbiosis_rate` still sits within ±0.12 of zero after 6000 ticks across three seeds — no drift toward parasitism or donation that the math would predict. Hypotheses: (1) most actual pairs are between organisms with similar rates (e.g. near-neutral plants rooted nearby) so the asymmetric-pair case that produces selection is rare; (2) the mutation rate + link magnitude combination is too weak vs lifespan; (3) parasites that do get a donor partner drive the donor to extinction fast, so both alleles drop. Follow-up: (a) run 20k+ ticks to see if drift resolves given enough time, (b) instrument the histogram of rates in linked pairs specifically, (c) consider a small metabolic discount for being in any active link (gives mutualists an a-priori reason to exist, like the social-sensing discount does for clustering).
**Accepted tradeoff:** the dynamic's machinery is live and visible in the Graphs tab (pair count climbing over time, organisms in the Inspect tab label themselves parasite/neutral/donor), but we're not seeing a population-level specialisation emerge yet. Shipping the tuning that works (more pairs) rather than waiting for the tuning that delivers the evolutionary outcome we hypothesised.

### Social sensing: metabolic discount, not behaviour reward
**Chosen:** organisms near same-species kin get a small (~5%) metabolic discount via formula `1 - (count/(count+5)) × 0.05`.
**Alternatives:** direct "reward for clustering" bonus (rejected as intellectually dishonest), no discount (rejected as not producing visible social behaviour).
**Why:** biologically justified as "reduced vigilance cost" — real animals in groups spend less energy watching for predators. Diminishing returns so infinite clustering isn't useful. This is a selection pressure gradient, not a behaviour prescription. Evolution decides whether to exploit grouping, ignore it, or find something better.
**Accepted tradeoff:** this is still a "thumb on the scale" — without it, social behaviour wouldn't emerge because the sim doesn't have enough latent group benefits (dilution of predation, etc. are too weak). We accept that a gentle nudge is the cost of seeing emergent sociality in reasonable time.

### Component presence for boolean state (Infection)
**Chosen:** `Infection` is a component; its presence means the organism is sick, its absence means healthy.
**Alternatives:** `Infection { is_infected: bool }` field, an `Option<Infection>` on a health struct.
**Why:** Bevy-idiomatic. Queries like `Query<..., Without<Infection>>` and `With<Infection>` read naturally and the ECS can skip healthy organisms entirely. Adding `Infection` to an entity is an actual state change, not a flag flip on an always-present blob.
**Accepted tradeoff:** adding/removing a component has slight overhead vs flipping a bool, but at 2000 organisms it's negligible.

### Grazing: an attack on a plant is a bite, not a kill
**Superseded 2026-09-23** by "Grazing through eat; an attack on a plant is a kill" below: bites now go through `eat`, and an attack on a plant is a kill. The bite size, the ledger booking and the one-bite-per-tick claim rule described here carry over.
**Chosen (2026-09-19, step 2 of `plans/2026-09-19-diet-axis.md`):** in `predation_system`, when an attacker's target is a photosynthesiser (`Genome::is_photosynthesiser`), the attack is a graze. The bite is `BITE_FRACTION` = 0.1 of the plant's current energy; the plant loses the bite and keeps its health, and is not marked `Killed`. The grazer is credited the bite times its `plant_efficiency()` through the same clamp path as any income; the undigested remainder goes to `digestion`. In the ledger `grazing` is a transfer flow like `symbiosis` (energy moved from plants to grazers, outside `net()`), because the bite is a transfer of the kept share plus a loss of the wasted share; booking it as income double-counted the kept share and put the residual at several units per tick until the first 300-tick run caught it. A plant takes one bite per tick under the same first-attacker-wins claim rule as kills. A graze passes the damage gate (claw strength against half the plant's armour, above 0.1) but not the size gate, so a small grazer can bite a large plant and plant armour still defends. Food items are digested at `plant_efficiency()` too, but their undigested share is not organism energy and does not enter the ledger, like the share a weak mouth already left. Kills are unchanged except that the killer's pyramid share is now digested at `animal_efficiency()` (see "Energy pyramid").
**Alternatives:** keep the size gate for grazes (rejected: it would make grazing a large-body strategy, which is the opposite of what a herbivore level needs); let a plant be bitten by every attacker in a tick (rejected for now: one bite per tick caps grazing pressure per plant and keeps the payout a fixed fraction, as the kill rule does; revisit in step 3 if grazers starve beside full plants); a new `DeathCause::Grazed` (deferred: a bite is a fraction so it never empties a plant on its own, and a plant grazed to the point where metabolism finishes it reads as starvation; a cause of its own is worth adding only if step 3 finds the starvation stat unreadable); gating grazes on a mouth segment rather than claws (not taken: the design doc says an attack becomes a graze, and `attack_power` mutates freely, so bite strength is cheap to evolve).
**Why the bite starts at a tenth:** the stated risk of the phase is plants going extinct before grazers specialise. A tenth of current energy per tick per grazer is a pressure, not an execution; step 3 owns the number.
**Opening result (2026-09-19, 5000 headless ticks, seeds 1, 3, 42, threshold 1.0):** grazing happened (9.6k, 16.4k and 14.4k bites against 214, 728 and 947 kills) and the ledger residual stayed under 0.001 per tick, but the diet mean ended at +0.02, -0.02 and 0.00, inside the neutral-drift baseline, and the non-plant population collapsed on every seed: 2, 8 and 20 organisms left against 1998, 1990 and 1980 plants. Seed 3 produced 37 grazers by tick 300 and they were gone by tick 3000. The flows say why. Photosynthesis brought in about 5.6M per run and the `max_organism_energy` clamp destroyed 3.8M to 4.2M of it, so plants sat saturated at the cap with nothing to spend on; food items brought non-plants 0.5M to 1.1M, a quarter of what the same items paid before digestion; grazing moved only 17k to 35k, because a bite needs the damage gate (claw strength above a tenth plus half the plant's armour) that founders with attack power in 0..0.1 rarely pass, and a passing bite of a saturated plant returns 12 energy of which a generalist keeps 3. Consumers therefore lost three quarters of their income the moment digestion arrived and gained almost nothing from grazing, and were gone before the diet trait could be selected. Three things follow for step 3, in the order the plan's open questions anticipated: widen the founder diet band so specialists exist from the start (the plan's first open question, now triggered); make bites reachable, by a larger `BITE_FRACTION`, a cheaper gate, or both; and decide the clamp (`energy-clamp-waste`), which is now the largest flow in the sim after photosynthesis itself.

### Diet trait: instrumented before it acts
**Chosen (2026-09-19):** the genome carries `diet` in -1..1 as its tenth scalar trait. It mutates (step 0.1 × strength, like symbiosis), crosses over per-trait, enters the compatibility distance, and is saved (`#[serde(default)]`, so old saves load as generalists at 0.0). Founders start in -0.2..0.2 like the other traits. `Genome::plant_efficiency` is `((1 - diet) / 2)²` and `animal_efficiency` is `((1 + diet) / 2)²`, so a generalist digests a quarter of each. Nothing reads either efficiency yet: this pull request is the instrument half of the project's instrument-then-dynamic-then-tune rule, and step 2 of `plans/2026-09-19-diet-axis.md` makes grazing and digestion act on them.
**Strategy labels:** `classify_strategy` moved from `clauvolution_sim` to `clauvolution_phylogeny`, next to the enum, so rendering and the UI call the same function instead of copying the rule. Plants first (`is_photosynthesiser`), then grazer at `diet ≤ -1/3`, hunter at `diet ≥ 1/3`, omnivore between. The old predator label (claw power above 0.5) is gone; hunter is a statement about digestion, and claws are the weapon, which shows up in the trait plot as attack.
**Alternatives:** land the trait and the mechanism together (rejected: the neutral-drift baseline below would never exist, and the threshold re-sweep would be tangled with the grazing balance); classify hunters by claws as before (rejected: the diet axis is what a hunter is once it acts, and a label that flips meaning between two pull requests is worse than one that is briefly uninformative); a wider founder band (deferred: the plan's open question, to revisit if step 2 shows nothing escaping the middle).
**Why the squared curve:** a linear split (`(1 - diet) / 2`) gives a generalist half of each and no reason to specialise. Squaring makes the middle a real cost, so the axis has two attractors and omnivory has to earn its place. The exponent is one of the knobs in step 3; it became `SimConfig::diet_efficiency_exponent` and was swept at 1.5 and 1.0 on 2026-09-24 (see "Digestion exponent as a knob").
**Neutral drift baseline (2026-09-19, 5000 headless ticks, threshold 1.0):** founders average `diet` within ±0.02 of zero on every seed. With nothing reading the trait, the population mean ended at +0.144 (seed 1), +0.003 (seed 3) and +0.044 (seed 42); across all nine runs of the threshold sweep below the final mean lay between +0.003 and +0.144 and every run ended on the carnivore side. No organism crossed ±1/3 in any run, so grazer and hunter counts were zero throughout and every non-plant read as an omnivore. The one-sided drift is consistent with hitchhiking on whichever lineages swept the plant population rather than selection, since no system reads the trait; it is noted here so step 2 does not mistake it for a signal. Step 2 is measured against these numbers: a mean beyond ±0.15, or any specialist count above zero, is more than drift produced here.

### One photosynthesiser predicate, two thresholds
**Chosen (2026-09-19):** `Genome::is_photosynthesiser()` (photo surface and rate above `PHOTOSYNTHESISER_RATE_THRESHOLD` = 0.2) is the identity rule for strategy classification, the plant density count in `photosynthesis_system`, niche construction, both minimap modes, sprite depth and colour, and the Inspect header. `Genome::can_photosynthesise()` (rate above `PHOTOSYNTHESIS_YIELD_THRESHOLD` = 0.01) gates the photosynthesis yield alone.
**Previous state:** the 0.2 rule was written out by hand at seven sites across three crates, niche construction used 0.1, and the yield gate 0.01, with nothing saying which differences were deliberate. Review findings `plant-classification-rule-copied` and `photosynthesis-gate-mismatch`; TODO `photosynthesiser-predicate`.
**Why two thresholds and not one:** identity and income are different questions. An organism at rate 0.05 with a photo surface is not a plant, but paying it for the sun it catches is how a lineage gets to plant-hood one mutation at a time; gating income at 0.2 would make photosynthesis unreachable by drift. Niche construction moved from 0.1 to the identity rule because terraforming is something plants do, and organisms between 0.1 and 0.2 are rare (founders start at 0.0..0.1 or 0.4..0.8).
**Accepted tradeoff:** the niche construction change is a behaviour change in principle. Checked on 2026-09-19 by building the branch with the old 0.1 niche threshold and nothing else changed: seed 3 gave 90% and 94% plants against 92% to 95% with the identity rule, seed 42 gave 63% against 56% to 73%. Invisible at 5000 ticks.
**Comparison with main at 5000 ticks (same day, threshold 1.0):** seed 1 gave 94% plants on the branch against 98% on main; seed 42 gave 56% and 73% against 55% (twice); seed 3 gave 92%, 94% and 95% against 77%, 77% and 87%. Seed 3 is the one seed where the branch runs sit above every main run, though main itself gave 63% on this seed the day before, and the niche threshold is ruled out above. The remaining difference is the tenth trait in the compatibility distance changing species boundaries, and with them who mates with whom; that is a consequence of adding any trait rather than of this one, and it lands inside the spread these seeds show across days. Same-seed runs started at different moments still diverged on both main and the branch, and two branch runs of seed 3 started at the same moment were bit-identical; that pattern was the wall-clock frame pacing later removed (see "Headless runs are deterministic" below).

### Feeding counters: who kills whom, and which output grazes
**Chosen (2026-09-23, step 1 of `plans/2026-09-21-pyramid-top.md`):** `PredationStats::feeding` is a `FeedingCounts` with grazes through `eat` and through `attack`, kills of consumers and of plants, kills by killers with `diet < 0` (all victims, and consumers only), and attack intents with no living photosynthesiser in reach. `PopulationHistory` diffs it into a per-second `PopSnapshot::feeding`, the way it does the energy ledger, so the history CSV, the Graphs tab (grazer kills of consumers as a line on the deaths-by-cause plot, grazes by output as their own plot) and the headless summary read the same numbers. The old `PredationStats::grazes` is now `feeding.grazes_attack`. Counting only: nothing reads the counters, and same-seed history CSVs on seeds 42, 3 and 7 at 5000 ticks are byte-identical to `main` with the new columns removed (`docs/audits/2026-09-23-pyramid-baseline/`).
**Grazer means `diet < 0` here,** not the strategy label's `diet <= -1/3`. The question the split has to answer is whether plant-leaning organisms stop killing consumers once they can graze with the mouth, and an omnivore at -0.2 digests plants better than animals and is as much a bystander killer as a labelled grazer. The label would leave those kills in the "other" column, which is where a hunter's kills belong.
**"No plant in reach" counts claimed plants.** A plant another attacker bit this tick is still in reach; leaving it out would make the figure depend on attack order within the tick. The figure is a statement about the world around the attacker, not about the contest.
**Alternatives:** a separate `TrophicStats` resource (rejected: every counter is observed inside `predation_system`, and step 2 adds the eat side in one place either way); per-strategy kill matrices (plant / grazer / omnivore / hunter killer against victim; deferred: sixteen cells for a question that two splits answer, and it can be added if step 5 needs hunter-specific rejection counts); putting the plant-in-reach test in the candidate loop only (rejected: it skips claimed targets, see above).
**Baseline:** grazer kills were 94% to 97% of all kills, predation 45% to 57% of all deaths, 67% to 71% of attack intents had no plant in reach, and every graze went through `attack`. Per-seed numbers are in the audit note.

### Grazing through eat; an attack on a plant is a kill
**Chosen (2026-09-23, step 2 of `plans/2026-09-21-pyramid-top.md`):** `grazing_system`, between `action_system` and `predation_system`, lets the `eat` output bite the nearest living photosynthesiser within `bite_reach × body size` (`--bite-reach`, shipped 1.0). The bite is `graze_bite` = plant energy × `bite_fraction` (0.3) × the eater's mouth bonus (1.0 with a mouth segment, `mouthless_bite_bonus` = 0.3 without, `--mouthless-bite`); the plant loses the bite and keeps its health, and the eater keeps it times its `plant_efficiency`. No claw, damage or size gate: the mouth is the grazer's tool. One bite per plant per tick, first eater wins, the kill rule. An eater that ate a food item in `action_system` this tick does not also bite (`AteFoodThisTick`): food items are free tissue and are eaten first, and an eater takes one meal per tick. Any organism may graze, plants included; the rule does not read the eater's identity (plants took 2% to 12% of eat bites). `predation_system` lost its graze branch: a photosynthesiser is prey under the same size and damage gates as anything else, and every kill is digested by tissue (`kill_digestion_efficiency`): plant victims at the killer's `plant_efficiency`, animal victims at `animal_efficiency × animal_efficiency_multiplier`. `FeedingCounts` gained eat bites by plants, plant kills by non-photosynthesisers and the energy those killers kept. No genome change; `SimConfig` is not saved beyond `terrain_seed`, so saves are unaffected.
**Where it lives:** a system of its own rather than a branch of `action_system`. Biting reads other organisms' energy, health and genome and writes a plant's energy, which `action_system`'s single mutable query cannot do while iterating; grafting that in would have meant a second pass, the spatial hash and the stats resources inside `action_system`. The cost is one small resource (`AteFoodThisTick`) that `action_system` fills, and `Entity` in its query.
**Alternatives:** keep the food-item reach, 3 × body size (rejected by the sweep below); keep plant armour as a defence against bites (not taken: it would keep the damage gate that made claws a grazing prerequisite, and plant defences are the plan's first follow-on); skip photosynthesisers in `predation_system` entirely (the plan's open question; rejected on the numbers below, and it is a rule about who may be attacked); let an eater take a food item and a bite in one tick (rejected: two meals per tick for one output).

The mouth bonus was the second knob: a mouthless share of 0 at reach 1.0 left seed 7 at 2 plants, and at reach 3.0 did not save seed 42's. A bite fraction of 0.1 at reach 3.0 did not save them either. 1.0 × body size ships: a bite needs contact. Full table in `docs/audits/2026-09-23-pyramid-graze-eat/`.

### Strike cost
**Chosen (2026-09-23, a follow-on to step 2 of `plans/2026-09-21-pyramid-top.md`):** in `predation_system`, every attacker (`attack > 0.5`) with at least one living organism within attack range pays `strike_cost × claw_power × body size` that tick (`strike_cost`, `--strike-cost`, shipped `DEFAULT_STRIKE_COST` = 1.0). Claw power × body size is the strike force the damage gate already reads, so a heavier strike costs more. The attacker pays whether the strike lands, bounces off a gate, or loses its target to an earlier attacker; firing with nobody in reach is free; an attacker killed in the same tick is not charged. The cost is booked to the ledger's `movement` flow (a strike is muscular work) and counted in `PredationStats::strikes` / `strike_energy`. `SimStats` gained founding-hunter deaths, summed age and oldest age, and the headless summary prints both. The rule reads neither the attacker's diet nor the target's identity.
**Why a strike and not every intent:** a founding hunter's random brain fires `attack` much of the time, usually at nothing. Charging for flailing would tax the organisms the plan is trying to keep alive for no selective gain, since a flail cannot kill anyone. Charging only when something is in reach puts the cost on exactly the act that produces a competition kill.
**Why claw power × size:** it is the physical force of the strike and it is already computed. A flat per-strike cost was the alternative; it would not distinguish a large clawed strike from a clawless tap, and a clawless attacker cannot pass the damage gate anyway, so under the chosen form it strikes for free and harmlessly.
**Why 1.0:** a killer keeps a tenth of the victim's energy times its digestion efficiency. For a 50-energy consumer, a hunter at diet +0.5 keeps about 2.8 and a grazer at diet -0.9 under 0.02. At 1.0 per unit of force a clawed hunter's kill still pays more than the strike across most founder strike forces, while a grazer's kill is a loss. At 3.0 and above the strike costs about as much as or more than a hunter's kill, which would close predation as an energy path for steps 4 to 6.

**Chosen (2026-09-23, step 5 of `plans/2026-09-21-pyramid-top.md`):** no rule change. The size gate in `predation_system` (attacker body size above 0.6 of the prey's) stays as it is, for consumer and plant prey alike, because it is not what stops hunters. What shipped is counters: `GateOutcomes` for three bands of consumer attackers (`diet >= 0` in `PredationStats::diet_nonneg_gates`; hunters in `FeedingCounts::hunter_gates`, which reaches the history CSV as seven appended columns; founding hunters in `PredationStats::founder_hunter_gates`), hunter intents and in-reach attacks by attacker age, and when founding hunters first fire and first have a consumer in reach. The headless summary prints them under `Consumer-prey gates`. Counting only: same-seed history CSVs on seeds 42, 3 and 7 at 5000 ticks are byte-identical to the step 4 runs with the new columns removed.
**Measurement (5000 ticks, seeds 42 / 3 / 7, hunter attacks with a consumer in reach):** 277 / 1,553 / 886 such attacks. Size gate alone 0.4% / 1.4% / 0.5%; damage gate alone 58% / 63% / 66%; both 22% / 11% / 8%; kills 18% / 24% / 26%. The size gate takes part in 8% to 22% of rejections and almost always alongside the damage gate; lifting it would have added 1, 22 and 4 kills over a whole run. The plan's condition for changing it, that it rejects most hunter attacks, is not met, so neither candidate (a gate derived from the computed damage, or one scaled by diet) was tried.

### Kill share by victim tissue
**Chosen (2026-09-23, toward step 6 of `plans/2026-09-21-pyramid-top.md`):** the single `kill_transfer_fraction` is split into `kill_transfer_animal` and `kill_transfer_plant`, read in `predation_system` through `SimConfig::kill_share(victim_is_plant)`: a photosynthesiser victim offers its killer the plant share, any other victim the animal share, before digestion as before. Both ship at 0.1, so default behaviour is unchanged (same-seed history CSVs on seeds 42, 3 and 7 at 5000 ticks are byte-identical to the base). Flags `--kill-transfer-animal` and `--kill-transfer-plant`; `--kill-transfer` stays and sets both, so older audit commands reproduce (its 1.0 runs are byte-identical to the hunter-payoff audit's), and a per-tissue flag beside it wins for its own share. New summary-only counters: plants killed by hunters and the energy they kept, and a grazer timeline (grazer size, armour, eat grazes every 500 ticks). Neither reaches the history CSV, which is what keeps it byte-identical.
**Why by tissue:** the hunter-payoff sweep (`docs/audits/2026-09-23-pyramid-hunter-payoff/`) found hunter lineages at `--kill-transfer 1.0` on two seeds with plants gone on all three, and the share paid for plant kills too. The split tells the two payoffs apart. It reads the victim's tissue, the same predicate `kill_digestion_efficiency` already branches on, and not the killer's diet, so it is a rule about what a body is made of, not a programmed preference.
**Sweep (animal share at 0.3 / 0.6 / 1.0, plant share 0.1, 5000 ticks, seeds 42 / 3 / 7):** hunters alive at 5000 on one seed at every share: seed 7 at 0.3 (one founder), seed 42 at 0.6 and 1.0 (descendant populations of 50 and 28). Plants were alive at 5000 on all nine runs (390 to 1,902), against 0 on all three seeds at 1.0 for both tissues. Plant floors after tick 1000 were 220 / 176 / 226 at 0.3, 11 / 95 / 145 at 0.6 and 37 / 33 / 2 at 1.0, against 320 / 256 / 497 at 0.1. Hunters killed many plants as bycatch (15k on seed 42 at 0.6 and 1.0, a third of their kills, keeping 0.05 to 0.1 energy each). Full table and grazer timelines in `docs/audits/2026-09-23-pyramid-tissue-share/`.
**Result: shipped at 0.1 / 0.1, the split kept as a knob.** The bar for a nonzero animal default was hunters at 5000 on two of three seeds with every plant floor at least about half the 0.1 baseline. No share meets the hunter half, and 0.6 and 1.0 miss the floor half on every seed, so the 15k check was not run. What the split does show: the plant extinction at the old full share came largely from paying plant kills in full (on seed 42 consumers kept 94,930 energy from 5,193 plant kills at 1.0 / 1.0, against 14,259 from 21,386 under the split), and taking that payoff away also took away seed 3's hunter lineage, which the counters do not explain (its hunters kept 233 energy from plants in the whole run). Floors still fall with the animal share, including on seed 3 at 0.6 and 1.0 where no hunter population formed. Grazer size drifts up in every run, hunters or not, and grazer armour rises tenfold only where hunters persist; bites were fewer, not more, in the windows where plants fell, so the eat-reach cascade the hunter-payoff note proposed is not supported, and the cause of the lower floors is not isolated.

**Accepted tradeoff:** one more `SimConfig` field and one more flag; `kill_transfer_fraction` is gone from the code, so older notes that name it mean both shares.

### Diet-band counters: income by tissue, deaths, births and crossings
**Chosen (2026-09-24, step 1 of `plans/2026-09-24-hunter-bridge.md`):** a `DietBandStats` resource counts, for consumers in six diet bands, organism-ticks, energy kept from food items, eat bites, consumer kills and plant kills, plant tissue taken before digestion, metabolism, movement and strike costs, births, and deaths by cause with the summed age at death. A four-by-four matrix counts births by the reproducing parent's strategy label and the child's; its off-diagonal cells are band crossings. `FeedingCounts::omnivore_gates` extends the step 5 gate outcomes with an omnivore band. `PopulationHistory` diffs the resource into a per-second `PopSnapshot::bands`, so the history CSV (121 trailing columns), the headless summary (a per-band block every 500 ticks, run totals by band and label, mean age at death by cause, the label matrix) and a Graphs plot of omnivore income by source against their costs read the same numbers. Counting only: nothing reads the counters, and same-seed history CSVs on seeds 42, 3, 7 and 99 at 5000 ticks are byte-identical to `main` with the new columns removed (`docs/audits/2026-09-24-hunter-bridge-baseline/`).
**Bands are the strategy labels split in half** at -2/3, 0 and +2/3 (`clauvolution_sim::diet_band`, which starts from `classify_strategy`), so the six bands nest exactly in the labels and every label-level figure is the sum of two bands. The plan suggested fifths; they cost the same and straddle the ±1/3 label edges, so neither label-level nor band-level figures would line up with the history's strategy counts. The split at 0 is the one the bridge question needs: a plant-leaning omnivore from an animal-leaning one.
**Where the counts are taken:** at the point each flow is booked. Food items and movement in `action_system`, bites in `grazing_system`, kills and strikes in `predation_system`, deaths and births in `death_system` and `reproduction_system`. Metabolism is paid under `par_iter_mut`, so its per-band share is read from each organism's `EnergyFlows` record in `ledger_system` before the record is cleared, and from a dying organism's record in `death_system`; an organism-tick is a tick in which the organism paid metabolism, so a newborn is first counted on its first full tick. Income is what the eater kept after digestion and before the energy clamp, as the ledger books it.
**The crossing is counted against the reproducing parent,** the one whose species, generation and position the child takes; the mate contributes only through crossover. Births with a mate of another label are counted too (`mixed_label_matings`: 86 to 315 per seed of 42k to 58k births in the baseline), which bounds how much the other convention could move the numbers.
**Plant tissue taken is counted as well as energy kept,** so realised plant efficiency (kept over taken) separates "found little to eat" from "digested little of it". Without it the structural-or-ecological question below could not be answered from the counts.
**Alternatives:** fold the counts into `PredationStats` (rejected: most of them are not about the attack path, and death, birth and ledger systems would all need it); sample per-band energy from genomes in `record_population_history` once a second (rejected: it would miss everything born and dead between samples, which for omnivores is most of them); track lineages by id for the crossing question (deferred: the label matrix answers whether omnivore lineages die out or drift, and lineage tracking is a larger instrument than the question needs).

### Omnivory's disadvantage is structural
**Decided (2026-09-24, step 1 of `plans/2026-09-24-hunter-bridge.md`):** the digestion curve binds; step 2 (the exponent as a knob, swept at 1.5 and 1.0) runs. On seeds 42, 3, 7 and 99 at 5000 ticks, labelled omnivores took 96.6% to 99.6% of their income from plant tissue. They took more plant tissue per organism-tick than grazers on every seed (0.82 to 1.37 against 0.54 to 0.64) and kept 0.27 to 0.38 of it against 0.85 to 0.89, so their income per organism-tick was 0.25 to 0.44 against grazers' 0.45 to 0.56. On seeds 42 and 7 omnivores kept less than they paid in every window, and on seed 3 from tick 500. Seed 99's omnivores matched grazers' income while plants were plentiful, by taking twice the tissue, and fell from 692 to 129 between ticks 1201 and 1801 as plants fell from 1440 to 701 and grazers stayed near 400: the shortage cut both bands' intake, and at a third of the digestion the omnivores went to break-even while grazers kept a surplus. No cause of death singles omnivores out (predation took a smaller share of omnivore than of grazer deaths on seed 99 in the same windows, disease a smaller share on every seed), and they die about as old as grazers. Omnivore lineages die out more than they drift: 3% to 25% of omnivore births became grazers and 1 to 3 per seed became hunters, while grazers' children mutating into the band replaced at least as many. 84% to 98% of omnivore attacks with a consumer in reach were stopped by the damage gate alone, so kills are not an income source for them (0.4% to 3.4% of income).
**Why this reading and not the ecological one:** the plan's ecological test was income matching grazers' per capita and deaths from something else. Income matched only on seed 99 and only while tissue per head was high; when it fell, the digestion ratio decided which band starved. Competition is the occasion, the curve is the reason. Numbers per seed in `docs/audits/2026-09-24-hunter-bridge-baseline/`.

### Digestion exponent as a knob
**Chosen (2026-09-24, step 2 of `plans/2026-09-24-hunter-bridge.md`):** the exponent of the digestion curve is `SimConfig::diet_efficiency_exponent`, default 2.0, overridable with `--diet-exponent` and refused below `MIN_DIET_EFFICIENCY_EXPONENT` (1.0) by the command-line check. Shipped at 2.0: neither swept value met the plan's bar.
**How it is threaded:** `Genome::plant_efficiency` and `animal_efficiency` take the exponent as an argument, and every caller passes the config value: `action_system` (food items), `grazing_system` (bites), `kill_digestion_efficiency` (which now takes `&SimConfig` for the exponent and the hunting multiplier together) and the Inspect panel. A parameter was the smallest change: the genome crate has no config access, the methods have four call sites, and a `*_with(exponent)` pair beside the old methods would have left a squared-only version for new code to call by mistake.
**Default runs are byte-identical.** At exactly 2.0 the curve multiplies (`h * h`); any other exponent calls `powf`. The first build used `powf(2.0)` throughout and its history CSVs differed from #78's on seed 42: `powf` differs from `h * h` in the last bit for some inputs. With the multiply, seeds 42 and 3 at 2000 ticks are byte-identical to the #78 branch, and `default_exponent_is_exactly_the_square` checks the two bit for bit across the axis.
**Why the floor at 1:** below 1 the two efficiencies sum to more than 1 in the middle, so a generalist out-digests a specialist and the axis loses its two ends. The plan rules it out; the check makes a typo such as `--diet-exponent 0.5` an error instead of a different world.
**The sweep** (seeds 42, 3, 7, 99; `docs/audits/2026-09-24-hunter-bridge-exponent/`): at 1.0 omnivores were alive after tick 3000 on three seeds (peaks 17, 2, 36) against one organism on one seed at 2.0, but on none at 5000, and at 15000 ticks the last omnivore was at 12301, 8521, 1621 and 6301. After 3000 they bred below replacement wherever they survived (3.44, 0.00, 0.67 births against 4.88, 5.15, 6.14 deaths per 1000 organism-ticks). Their realised plant efficiency rose from 0.27-0.38 to 0.56-0.60, but grazers moved inward (consumer diet -0.57 to -0.82 at 5000, from -0.91 to -0.95) and kept 0.77-0.83, so the gap a shortage acts on narrowed without closing. At 1.5 the last omnivore was at 3991, 1741, 751, 2341. No hunter was alive at 5000 at either value; at 15000 and 1.0 seed 3 had one, which earned nothing after tick 3000. At 1.0 plant-fed founders near the hunter edge grew early (to 266 on seed 42, 183 on 99) and died out by about tick 1050, taking 1.5% and 0.9% of their income from animal tissue, the design table's apex that grazes. Plants were lost on seed 7 at 1.0 (none from tick 2191) and fell to one on seed 3 at 1.5.
**Alternatives:** ship 1.0 (rejected: fails the plan's bar on every clause, and costs seed 7 its plants); a flat-topped curve, the plan's option (c) (not run: its trigger was "no intermediates alive after 3000", which 1.0 narrowly avoids, and the reading suggests any curve under which a specialist out-digests a generalist leaves the neighbouring grazer band ahead); `*_with(exponent)` variants (rejected above).
**Accepted tradeoff:** one more `SimConfig` field and flag, and an `if` on the hot digestion path to keep default runs identical.

## Brains & evolution

### NEAT with 26 inputs / 9 outputs (no hard-coded behaviour)
**Chosen:** each organism has a per-organism neural network with specific labelled inputs and outputs, but no behaviour is pre-programmed.
**Alternatives:** scripted behaviour trees, finite state machines, heuristic AI.
**Why:** the core goal of the project — watch evolution discover behaviour. Scripted behaviour wouldn't be evolution, it'd be a game.
**Accepted tradeoff:** early-generation organisms behave poorly until selection produces useful circuits. Initial-diversity seeding (30% photosynthesisers) compensates by guaranteeing *some* strategy works out of the gate.

### Nearest-eater inputs
**Chosen (2026-09-23, step 4 of `plans/2026-09-21-pyramid-top.md`):** four brain inputs, 22 to 25, for the nearest non-photosynthesiser (`!Genome::is_photosynthesiser`) within the organism's sense range: direction x and y (a unit vector), closeness `1 - dist / sense_range`, and size ratio `min(its size / own size, 2) / 2`. The encoding is the nearest-organism inputs' (4 to 7) exactly, including all four reading 0 when nothing qualifies. They are found in the same neighbour scan as the nearest organism, so the cost is one predicate per neighbour. `NUM_INPUTS` went from 22 to 26; the inputs are appended after the bias so every older input keeps its index. The Inspect brain view labels them `eater dx`, `eater dy`, `eater near` and `eater size`, and its height grew from 300 to 340 px so the labels keep their spacing.
**Alternatives:** make the existing nearest-organism inputs skip plants (changes what every existing brain sees, and a grazer loses its only organism-direction input to a plant); a per-diet "nearest prey" that reads the sensing organism's diet to pick what counts as prey (a programmed preference, against the project's principle; this input reads only what the other organism is); a nearest-eater photo hint or signal (not needed to steer; kept to the four the plan names).
**Why:** plants are about half the population, so the nearest organism is usually a plant and a hunter's brain had nothing that pointed at an animal.
**Result: founding-hunter lifetime did not measurably change.** Mean age at death of founding hunters at 5000 ticks, base then with the inputs: 270 to 256 (seed 42), 241 to 263 (seed 3), 249 to 265 (seed 7). A control build with the four inputs held at 0, which has the same founders as the shipped build, gave 262, 228 and 258, so changing the founder draws alone moves the mean by 8 to 13 ticks, as much as the inputs appear to. No hunter was alive at 5000 on any seed; kills by diet >= 0 killers were 258, 547 and 450 against 874, 330 and 402. Plants and grazers persist and cycle on all three seeds, with plant floors after tick 1000 of 320, 256 and 497 against 48, 184 and 622. The plan's criterion measures founders, whose random brains rarely wire these inputs to movement; the inputs can only help a hunter lineage through selection, which founders dying at about 250 ticks do not reach. Full table and the control in `docs/audits/2026-09-23-pyramid-nearest-eater/`.
**Accepted tradeoff:** every founder's brain draw changed with `NUM_INPUTS`, so same-seed runs are not comparable with runs from before this change. The inputs stay: they cost nothing measurable in pyramid health and give any later hunter lineage something to steer at.

### Species classification: trait-led distance, threshold 1.0 with 1.3× hysteresis
**Chosen:** organisms are classified by `Genome::compatibility_distance`, threshold 1.0 to join a species and 1.3 (1.3× hysteresis) to stay in one, re-evaluated every 5 seconds. The distance has four terms, each roughly 0..1 before weighting: excess connections / N and disjoint connections / N at 0.5 each, mean weight difference of matching connections at 0.5, and a body term at 1.0. The body term is the mean over the scalar traits (nine at the time, ten since `diet` was added on 2026-09-19) of `|a - b| / span`, where `span` is the trait's mutation clamp range (`SCALAR_TRAIT_BOUNDS` in `clauvolution_genome`), so one trait at opposite ends of its range contributes `1 / SCALAR_TRAIT_COUNT` and the whole term lies in 0..1.
**Previous values (2026-04 to 2026-09-18):** c1 = 1.0, c2 = 1.0, c3 = 0.4, body term `× 0.5` on raw trait deltas with ad hoc per-trait scalars (sense range × 0.01, disease resistance × 0.5, symbiosis × 0.3), threshold 1.3. The raw body term could exceed 2 on its own (speed factor spans 2.8, body size 1.7), so species were trait-led by accident and the earlier threshold sweep (2.0 → 1.5 → 1.3 → 1.0, recorded below) was tuning that unnamed lever. Review finding `compatibility-body-term-unbounded`.
**Alternatives:** brain-led (NEAT terms at 1.0, body at 0.5); balanced (all terms equal). Rejected in `docs/design/simulation-rules.md`: a species should be a way of making a living that can be seen from outside, and the diet and biome traits planned for phases 1 and 2 will carry that meaning. The brain terms stay so a behavioural split can still become a species.

**Accepted tradeoff:** the count is steep in the threshold near 1.0, so any new trait (phases 1 and 2 each add some) will need the sweep repeated, as the design doc's risks section already says. Species now split on body traits before brains, so two lineages with identical bodies and different behaviour need a larger brain divergence to separate than before. Hysteresis stays at 1.3× so an organism stays in its current species up to distance 1.3, preventing flip-flopping at the threshold. The threshold sweeps above were run before the stay rule, when membership churned every pass, so their counts are indicative only for the current code; the 5000-tick counts after the change land in the same 16 to 23 range. Seed 42 still forms few species, which is the distance ceiling rather than the classification rule.

### Crossover blends each scalar trait with its own factor
**Chosen:** `Genome::crossover` draws a fresh blend factor `t` for each scalar trait (body size, speed, sense range, aquatic adaptation, photosynthesis rate, armour, attack power, disease resistance, symbiosis rate, and since 2026-09-19 diet) and sets the child's trait to `a × t + b × (1 - t)`. Body segments and brain crossover are unchanged.
**Alternatives:** one shared `t` for all traits (previous behaviour); uniform crossover (each trait copied whole from one parent); child always takes the fitter parent's traits.
**Why:** with one shared factor every child lies on the straight line between its parents in trait space, so sexual reproduction could never combine parent A's speed with parent B's armour. Independent factors let a mating produce trait combinations neither parent had, which is the point of sex in the sim. Blending rather than copying whole keeps children near their parents so speciation is not disturbed by crossover alone. Resolves review finding `crossover-single-blend-factor`.
**Accepted tradeoff:** children of very different parents scatter across a hyperrectangle instead of a line, so more of them land in untested trait combinations; mates are same-species by construction, so the parents are already close and the scatter is small.

### Species naming — habitat + descriptor + strategy, children inherit two of three
**Chosen:** three-word names like "Swamp Dwarf Moss" (habitat = Swamp, descriptor = Dwarf, strategy noun = Moss). Child species inherit their parent's habitat and strategy noun, only varying the descriptor.
**Alternatives:** random names, Latin binomial generation, ID numbers.
**Why:** evolutionary trees are more readable when related species have related names. Three-word structure mimics real taxonomy enough to be legible.
**Accepted tradeoff:** word-list approach means occasional collisions — two unrelated lineages might happen to share a name by virtue of similar traits and modular arithmetic on species ID. Resolved on 2026-09-20; see the next entry.

### Species names are unique among living species: mixed id, longer lists, then a walk
**Chosen:** `pick` mixes the species id with a splitmix64 finalizer, salted per word slot, so habitat, descriptor and noun vary independently; the lists grew from 5 words to 8 (habitat, noun) and 10 (descriptor), plus 24 trait-neutral colour and pattern descriptors as overflow. `PhyloTree::record_species` then checks the candidate against every living species' name and, on a clash, walks the descriptor list (the trait bucket first, then the overflow words) before appending the lowest unused number to the first candidate as a last resort. A child of a numbered parent drops the number when it inherits the noun. Extinct species do not block a name, so a name can recur across a run's history but never among what is on screen at once.
**Alternatives:** larger word lists or a better hash alone; a numeric suffix on every collision; a global counter per name; unique random names with no trait meaning.
**Why:** the old `id % 5` used the same index for all three words, so a trait bucket had five names in total, and a child kept two of them, leaving five names for a whole lineage. In a simulated 200-species tree 150 to 175 of the 200 shared a name; in a `--headless 3000 --seed 42` run 11 of 27 living species did. The hash and the longer lists alone are not enough, because a child varies only its descriptor: at about 40 living species 11 to 29 still shared a name with their first candidate across five seeds. The walk past living names is what brings collisions to zero (0 of 39 living in the same headless run, 0 of ~40 across five simulated seeds, no numbers needed), and it costs one scan of the phylo nodes per new species, which happens a few dozen times a run. Names are stored as strings in the save, so old saves load unchanged.
**Accepted tradeoff:** a species' name now depends on which relatives were alive when it appeared, so it is not a pure function of traits and id, and a walked descriptor may say less about the species than the first choice would have (an overflow colour word says nothing). Numbers remain possible when more than 34 living relatives share a habitat and noun; the stress test with all 200 species alive needed up to 48.

### Convergent evolution is chronicled by a per-strategy high-water mark
**Chosen:** `ConvergenceHighWater` in `clauvolution_sim` holds the highest independent-lineage count already logged for each `SpeciesStrategy`. `species_classification_system` writes a "Convergent evolution!" line only when `detect_convergence` returns a count above that mark. The map is a plain resource and is not saved.
**Alternatives:** scan existing chronicle text for a matching line (previous behaviour); persist the map in the save file; log on every change in count, downward as well as upward.
**Why:** the text scan compared against a substring the logged text never contained, so the same line was written on every five-second pass and made up almost half of a 3000-tick chronicle. A map keyed on the strategy makes the decision explicit and constant-time, and a pure `record` method pins it with a unit test. Logging only new highs keeps the chronicle to milestones rather than a running count.
**Accepted tradeoff:** the map starts empty when a save is loaded, so the first classification pass after a load restates each strategy's current convergence once. Persisting it would add a save-format field for a single repeated line per load; not worth it.

### Convergent evolution counts recorded strategy changes, not founders
**Chosen:** `PhyloTree::detect_convergence` counts, for each strategy, the distinct ancestral species it evolved from. For each living species with at least `CONVERGENCE_MIN_POPULATION` (10) members it walks up the tree while the strategy stays the same; the first ancestor with a different strategy is the departure point, and a species whose line keeps its strategy back to a founder is not counted. Species sharing a departure point count once. A strategy is reported when it has at least two departure points, and the chronicle high-water mark above is unchanged.
**Previous behaviour:** every species with the strategy counted, grouped by the ancestor ten steps up (or the founder, if nearer). Founder species have no parent, so each founder species was its own "independent lineage", and nothing had to evolve for a line to be written.
**Why:** "N independent lineages evolved X" should mean X arose N separate times, which only a recorded change of strategy in the tree can show; the founding population's spread of strategies is where evolution starts, not something it did.
**Measurements (2026-09-23, `--headless 3000`, seeds 42 and 3, 20 classification passes each, a temporary instrument printing every pass):** before the change the first pass (tick 151) reported convergence on both seeds, and every detection on both runs was made of founder species that still had their founding strategy. Seed 42 formed no species after the founding pass in 3000 ticks, so all 11 chronicle lines it wrote (all by tick 1051, photosynthesis climbing from "3 independent lineages" to 20) were the founders' diversity. Seed 3 wrote 7 lines by tick 751, all founders; "2 independent lineages evolved hunting" was two founder hunter species. The one real strategy change in either run was on seed 3 at tick 2701, when three grazer species split off one photosynthesiser founder in the same pass. After the change neither run reports convergence on any pass: that event has one departure point, which is one origin, not convergence. Per-call cost was 2 to 5 microseconds before and 1 to 4 microseconds after, against a tick that costs milliseconds, and the call runs once per five-second pass. Same-seed `--dump-history` CSVs and summaries are identical before and after on both seeds, as expected for a change that only reads the tree.
**Alternatives:** a warm-up delay before the first detection (founder species persist for thousands of ticks, so the same founder-only lines would appear after the delay; on seed 42 the founders were still the whole tree at tick 3000); a cheaper scan (at a few microseconds there is nothing to save); leaving it alone (a milestone that fires on the first pass of every run teaches the viewer to ignore it); counting each switching species separately (the three seed-3 siblings would have read as "3 independent lineages evolved grazing" when they were one population drifting across the line); keeping lineage identity at the founder (misses two separate switches inside one founder's line, which is the case the chronicle line is for).
**Accepted tradeoff:** a species' strategy is read off its founding genome when it forms and is never updated, so a species whose members drift across a threshold only shows the change when a new species splits off. Early runs now usually report no convergence at all, which is the honest answer; the detector can only fire once two separate switches to the same strategy have each produced a species of ten or more. Parent links are only as good as species ids; the next entry covers how ids are issued.

### Species ids are issued once, from the phylo tree
**Chosen (2026-09-23):** `species_classification_system` starts each pass at `PhyloTree::next_species_id()`, one past the highest id the tree holds, living or extinct, and still raises it past any living organism's id. The tree never removes a node and the save writes every node, so the tree is already the record of every id ever issued; no new resource or save field is needed.
**Previous behaviour:** the counter started at one past the highest id among living organisms. Once the highest-numbered species died out, the next new species got its id; `record_species` saw the id already in the tree and returned early, so the newcomer took over the dead species' node, with its name, parent, strategy and birth tick, and `update_populations` marked the node alive again. The chronicle's "New species" line then carried the dead species' name, and the Phylo tab showed a founder-era species coming back to life.
**Measurements (2026-09-23, `--headless 5000`, seeds 42 and 3, a temporary instrument printing each classification pass and each reused id):** seed 42 formed no species after the founding pass (60 founders, 25 alive at tick 5000), so it could not reuse an id; seed 3 formed 13 species after its 21 founders and reused one id, at tick 4801, when a new species took id 32 from a species born at tick 4201 and extinct at 4351. After the change that species became node 34. Same-seed `--dump-history` CSVs and summaries are byte-identical before and after on both seeds. That is expected: ids are only ever compared for equality (same-species mating, the hysteresis check), and the reused id never belonged to a living species, so which organisms share a species is unchanged and only the label and the tree differ.
**Alternatives:** a monotonic counter in a resource, persisted in the save (a second record of the same fact that has to be kept in step with the tree, a new `SaveState` field, and a default derived from the saved nodes anyway); leaving the id scheme and making `record_species` replace an existing extinct node (the old node's history, and any child's parent link to it, would be overwritten).
**Accepted tradeoff:** each pass scans the tree's keys once, a few dozen to a few hundred entries every 150 ticks. A save without phylo nodes would start from an empty tree, and extinct ids recorded only in chronicle links could then be reissued; current saves cannot omit the field.

## World & environment

### Seasons: 60-second year, sinusoidal light & food regen
**Chosen:** a full spring/summer/autumn/winter cycle every 60 real-time seconds.
**Alternatives:** longer cycles (more realistic but slow to watch), no seasons, seasonal on/off only.
**Why:** creates regular environmental pressure on a timescale where you can actually observe adaptation within one viewing session. 60 seconds is short enough that you'll see winter affect populations multiple times in a run.
**Accepted tradeoff:** not a lifetime pressure — organisms can't "adapt" within one season. Multi-generational pressure needs long-term climate shift (which is on the roadmap).

### Terrain noise ranges: elevation -1..1, moisture 0..1
**Chosen:** `generate_noise_map` normalises each noise map to 0..1. `TileMap::generate` remaps only elevation to -1..1; moisture keeps 0..1.
**Alternatives:** normalise both to -1..1 (what shipped originally), remap moisture with `(m + 1) / 2` at each consumer.
**Why:** elevation needs a signed range because water is "below zero" (DeepWater under -0.3, ShallowWater under -0.05). Nothing about moisture is signed. Every consumer assumes 0..1: the biome thresholds in `Tile::from_elevation_moisture` (Sand/Rock below 0.25, Forest above 0.6), the vegetation carrying capacity `nutrients × moisture` in `tile_dynamics_system`, the `min(1.0)` clamp in `niche_construction_system`, and the ice age's `moisture *= 0.7`. With -1..1, roughly half the land had negative moisture, so its carrying capacity was negative and vegetation decayed to zero with no regrowth; Forest was confined to the top fifth of the range; and the ice age made dry (negative) tiles wetter, since scaling a negative number toward zero raises it. On the default 512² world at seed 42, Sand went from about 65% of land to 16% and Grassland from 12% to 46% once the range was corrected.
**Accepted tradeoff:** the biome thresholds were left at 0.25 and 0.6, so Rock (dry and above elevation 0.6) is now rare to absent on some seeds; it was only 0.2% of land before. Any retuning of thresholds, `PHOTO_OUTPUT_MULTIPLIER`, or initial seeding in response to the greener world belongs to a separate tuning pass after the attractor audit is re-run. Saves regenerate terrain from the seed, so saves made before this change load with a different biome layout.

### Per-biome seeding: founders placed by biome area, starting below their reproduction threshold
**Chosen:** `spawn_initial_population` places the 400 founders on land in proportion to each biome's tile count (Sand, Grassland, Forest, Rock; water is not a founding habitat), with a floor of 5% of the founders for any biome holding at least 1% of the counted land, paid for by the most populous biomes. Every founder starts at 90% of its own body-scaled reproduction threshold, `reproduction_energy_threshold × (0.5 + body_size × 0.5)`, so on the default config a plant founder (body 0.3 to 0.8) starts at 41 to 57 energy and a minimal founder (body 0.5 to 1.5) at 47 to 79. The genome split is unchanged: the first third of founders are photosynthesisers, the rest minimal genomes, and the biome slots are shuffled so both kinds land in every biome. The founder counts per biome and per strategy are logged at startup and printed at the top of the headless summary.
**Alternatives:** leave the founding boom (uniform random placement, energy at `max_organism_energy × 0.5`); stage founders by trophic level, plants first; a fixed founder energy equal to the nominal threshold of 70; a per-biome founder count independent of area.
**Why:** the attractor audit found that the regime is decided in the first few sim-seconds, before anything has adapted, and the design doc (`docs/design/simulation-rules.md`) asks for an opening that is colonisation rather than a feeding frenzy on free energy. Staging by trophic level would programme the pyramid into the opening. Placing by area rather than a fixed count per biome keeps founder density roughly even across land, which is the same starting point every biome would reach under uniform placement minus the founders that used to land in water (about 35% of the map on seed 42); the floor exists so a small biome such as Rock is not empty by chance when it does appear. On energy, the pre-change value was `max_organism_energy × 0.5 = 60`, which is below the nominal threshold of 70 but above the effective threshold for most founders, because `reproduction_system` scales the threshold by body size (45 to 63 for plant founders, 52 to 70 for most minimal ones). Founders could therefore reproduce on tick one. The nominal 70 would have been a raise, so the effective threshold is used; 90% of it means the first birth has to be paid for with roughly one food item's worth of earned energy.
**Accepted tradeoff:** the founding boom is slowed, not removed. ### Deep water 10× movement cost
**Chosen:** land organisms crossing deep water pay 10× energy cost per step.
**Alternatives:** soft gradient, impassable barrier, no penalty.
**Why:** creates real geographic isolation between landmasses so allopatric speciation actually happens. A soft gradient wouldn't reliably isolate populations; a hard barrier would feel like a wall.
**Accepted tradeoff:** some aquatic-adapted organisms cross freely which can feel odd — but that's the selection pressure the trait exists to respond to.

## UI & rendering

### Single tabbed right panel vs multiple always-visible panels
**Chosen:** one right-side panel with tabs (Inspect / Phylo / Graphs / Chronicle / Events / Help). You see one tab's content at a time.
**Alternatives:** multiple fixed panels (original layout, plus the bevy_egui migration initially), floating windows, dockable panels.
**Why:** the world view is the primary content — maximise its screen area. Related info is usually consulted separately (graphs for trends, chronicle for events, phylo for species). Rarely needed simultaneously. Simpler to build and tune across window sizes.
**Accepted tradeoff:** can't see graphs AND chronicle at the same time. We can add an "eject to window" button later if that becomes a real need. It hasn't.

### egui_plot for charts instead of ASCII sparklines
**Chosen:** real line charts via `egui_plot` with pan/zoom/legend.
**Alternatives:** keep ASCII sparklines, roll our own chart rendering.
**Why:** egui_plot is built for this exact use case, gives us multi-series charts, interactive zoom, tooltip values for free. Sparklines were cute but couldn't show per-cause-death breakdowns usefully.
**Accepted tradeoff:** pulls in another dependency version-locked to bevy_egui.

### egui pointer-capture check in PreUpdate
**Chosen:** `UiInputState.pointer_over_ui` is populated in PreUpdate by querying egui's `ctx.is_pointer_over_area()`.
**Alternatives:** check in the same system that runs the panel (too early — side panel not drawn yet that frame), check in PostUpdate (too late — Update systems already consumed inputs).
**Why:** egui retains layout state across frames. At the start of a frame, egui still knows where its panels were drawn *last* frame. For cursor-based gating that's accurate enough — the user moves the cursor 1 frame before they click/scroll.
**Accepted tradeoff:** 1-frame lag on pointer-over-UI detection. At 60fps that's 16ms — imperceptible.

### Bevy gizmos for trails and infection halos
**Chosen:** use `gizmos.linestrip_2d()` / `gizmos.circle_2d()` for visual overlays.
**Alternatives:** per-segment entities, per-organism child meshes, custom shader.
**Why:** gizmos batch into a single draw call automatically. For 2000 potential trails and N infected organisms, gizmos are essentially free to render. Per-entity approaches would create entity-management headaches (spawn on infect, despawn on recover, sync per tick).
**Accepted tradeoff:** gizmos are "for debug" in Bevy's docs and have simple visual options. For our case (faint lines, simple circles) that's fine.

### Photosynthesisers render at z=0.3, active organisms at z=1.0
**Chosen:** plants render behind active organisms, no outline. Actives render with outline.
**Alternatives:** all at same z, all with outlines, all without outlines.
**Why:** visual clarity. When you're watching the sim, plants are "ground cover" and actives are "the things moving around". Separating z-orders makes the screen parseable.
**Accepted tradeoff:** in a plant-heavy world the active organisms can get obscured if they walk into dense foliage — but that matches the biology.

## Architecture & performance

### Unified event bus (WorldEventRequest) for keyboard + UI
**Chosen:** both hotkeys and UI buttons emit the same `WorldEventRequest` event; one system consumes.
**Alternatives:** duplicate the effect logic for keyboard vs UI, shared resource flags instead of events.
**Why:** one code path means one place for cooldown logic, chronicle logging, and effect application. Adding a new triggering mechanism (e.g. REST API, scheduled events) just means another event source — no logic duplication.
**Accepted tradeoff:** requires slightly more plumbing (defining events, reading events) than direct function calls. Worth it for the symmetry.

### Virtual time pause instead of near-zero timestep for pausing
**Chosen:** pausing the sim calls `Time::<Virtual>::pause()`. Unpausing calls `unpause()`.
**Alternatives:** set `Time::<Fixed>` timestep to something huge so no ticks fire (what we had first — broken).
**Why:** the original approach let virtual time keep accumulating while paused, which filled the fixed timestep accumulator. On unpause, Bevy tried to "catch up" by running thousands of ticks, freezing the app. Pausing virtual time halts accumulation entirely.
**Accepted tradeoff:** none worth noting — this is just the right way to do it in Bevy. Pause and relative speed are independent fields on `Time<Virtual>`, so pausing at 16× and unpausing resumes at 16×.

### Frustum culling in the render systems, not by Bevy's built-in
**Chosen:** in `sync_organism_transforms` and `sync_food_transforms`, check each entity's position against the camera viewport and set `Visibility::Hidden` if off-screen. Margin-padded to prevent pop-in. The viewport is the primary window's logical size times the projection scale (`visible_world_rect`), which is what the default `ScalingMode::WindowSize` projection shows; the infection halos and the minimap's viewport box use the same rectangle. Until September 2026 all three hardcoded a 1920×1080 window, so any other window size culled visible organisms at the edges.
**Alternatives:** rely on Bevy's built-in visibility culling (doesn't apply to 2D Mesh2d entities by default), render everything (wasted GPU), draw order spatial partition.
**Why:** a 4-float-comparison per entity is trivial vs rendering cost for thousands of off-screen organisms. Big win at 2000 organisms.
**Accepted tradeoff:** a small margin (~20px scaled) is used to prevent entities flickering at the camera edge — means we draw slightly more than strictly necessary.

### Headless mode: `--speed N` virtual-time multiplier, capped by CPU
**Chosen:** `--headless` runs with `MinimalPlugins` and `--speed N` (default 10) scales `Time::<Virtual>::relative_speed`. FixedUpdate fires as fast as the CPU can sustain. Since 2026-09-20 the headless clock advances by exactly one 30 Hz timestep of real time per frame (`TimeUpdateStrategy::ManualDuration`), so `--speed N` means N ticks per frame and wall-clock time never enters the run; see "Headless runs are deterministic" below. Before that, the clock followed the wall clock and `max_delta` clamped the catch-up.
**Same mechanism in the GUI:** the `[` / `]` speed keys write `SimSpeed::multiplier`, as does headless `--speed`, and `sim_speed_system` applies that to `Time<Virtual>` in both modes. The fixed timestep stays at 30 Hz everywhere. Until September 2026 the GUI instead rescaled the fixed timestep (`set_timestep_hz(30 × multiplier)`), which shrank the delta every virtual-time timer saw inside `FixedUpdate`: at 16× the species classifier ran every 2400 ticks instead of every 150, the 1 Hz history sampler every 480 ticks, and "sim time" in the header fell 16× behind headless for the same tick count. Measured at 4× with a scripted tour: classification passes at sim time 18s/38s/58s/1m18s before, 5s/10s/15s/20s after. `--species-threshold` was likewise only applied in the headless startup chain; it now applies in both. Both modes running the same per-tick simulation is what makes headless sweeps a valid stand-in for what is watched. Consequence for `--script` tours: `at_seconds` is virtual time, so after a `set_speed` action the remaining triggers arrive `multiplier` times sooner in wall-clock terms.
**Alternatives considered:** tiny fixed timestep (changes the semantic meaning of one tick for virtual-time timers like species classification), manual schedule invocation (breaks timers entirely), keeping it at 1× wall-clock (what v1 did).
**Why this is where we landed:** relative_speed leaves every sim timer semantically intact (classification still runs every 5 virtual-seconds, seasons still 60 virtual-seconds, etc.) — only the mapping from virtual to wall-clock changes. Measured speedup is 2.8× (50s → 17.6s for 1500 ticks on M4 Max). Beyond `--speed 5` further multiplication does nothing because the CPU is the floor at ~85 ticks/sec.
**What was wrong with the v1 assumption:** I previously claimed headless couldn't go faster than 30Hz because "the sim is CPU-bound at 30Hz." Half right. The sim IS CPU-bound at ~85 ticks/sec, but v1 was bottlenecked on Bevy's virtual-time pacing at 30Hz wall-clock — which is a configurable thing, not a compute thing. Fixing the pacing gets us to the real CPU ceiling.
**Accepted tradeoff:** speedup caps at whatever your CPU sustains per-tick, which depends heavily on organism count and system count. For further speedup we still need less per-tick compute (more Rayon, GPU compute for brains, or fewer organisms). The earlier note here that same-seed runs diverge after ~50 ticks because of the task pool was wrong; the cause was the wall-clock pacing this entry introduced, and it is resolved below.

### Headless runs are deterministic
**Chosen:** a headless run is a function of the seed and the flags, at any `--speed` and any `CLAU_WORKERS`. Three changes make it so. `run_headless` sets `TimeUpdateStrategy::ManualDuration` to one 30 Hz timestep, so each frame advances the clock by a fixed amount, `--speed N` means N ticks per frame, and wall-clock time never enters the run. `food_regeneration_system` and `tile_dynamics_system`, previously registered in `FixedUpdate` by `WorldPlugin` with no ordering, run inside the `SimTick` chain directly after `tick_counter_system`. `update_body_plans`, previously once per frame in `PostUpdate`, runs in `FixedUpdate` after `SimTick`, as does the headless tick counter.
**What the docs blamed, and what it was:** this document, the roadmap and the `SimRng` doc comment blamed Bevy's parallel task pool and HashMap iteration order. Neither was involved. Every `par_iter_mut` system writes only the iterated entity's own components, and every std `HashMap` or `HashSet` in the tick is used for lookups or for counts that do not depend on order. The cause was frame pacing. `Time<Virtual>` followed `Time<Real>`, so the number of ticks a frame ran depended on how long the previous frame took, and `update_body_plans`, running once per frame, inserted `BodyPlan` on that frame's newborns after a frame-dependent number of ticks. Inserting a component moves the entity to another archetype, archetype layout is Query iteration order, and the serial systems resolve first-come-first-served contests and consume `SimRng` in that order: birth admission, disease rolls, mate choice, first attacker wins. Bevy clamps a frame's real delta at 100 ms, so once a tick cost more than about 3 ms every frame ran exactly 30 ticks and the sequence was fixed; only the cheap frames just after startup let the wall clock in. That is why loaded machines, single-worker runs and simultaneous runs tended to match while idle machines did not, and why the 2026-09-18 audit saw the four seeds whose first run was in the first batch after launch diverge.
**Evidence** (seed 42, sequential runs on a machine also running other jobs, so wall times are noisy; "identical" means the history CSVs have the same md5 and the summaries differ only in the timing line). Before, 1000 ticks: two default-pool runs differed from the first 1 Hz snapshot (tick 31), taking 81.7 s and 47.7 s; two `CLAU_WORKERS=1` runs were identical (61.6 s and 60.4 s; single-worker ticks are slow enough to sit in the clamp regime from the start). Manual clock only: six default-pool runs and one `CLAU_WORKERS=1` run identical, so the pool size is irrelevant; `--speed 1`, `10` and `30` gave three different runs, each reproducible, because the frame partition still reached the sim through body plans. Wall clock restored with `update_body_plans` disabled: two of three runs were identical to each other and to a manual-clock build with `update_body_plans` disabled at speeds 1, 10 and 30, which isolates body plans as the only per-frame path into the sim. Everything inside the tick: speeds 1, 10 and 30 identical. At 5000 ticks with the manual clock alone, a default-pool pair diverged at snapshot tick 4891 (identical for the 163 snapshots before) while a `CLAU_WORKERS=1` pair was identical and matched one of the two, so that residual is thread-timing dependent; it is what chaining the two unordered world systems is for, and the outcome of that check is recorded in the next paragraph.
**Residual check:** with everything inside the tick, two default-pool 5000-tick pairs, four runs, were identical (`67be692e…`, 4601 births, wall times 281 s to 340 s). The unordered systems were the only thread-timing-dependent placement in the tick, so this is the expected result; the residual showed in one of three 5000-tick runs before the change, so four matched runs are good but not conclusive evidence, and the CI determinism probe accumulates the rest. If a mismatch ever appears there again, the suspects are thread-timing ones (a system added to `FixedUpdate` outside `SimTick`, or a parallel system that writes shared state), not the wall clock.
**Alternatives:** forcing one compute worker (the single-worker matches were the clamp regime, not the pool; the fix is worker-count independent, and one worker costs the parallel systems); sorting query results by Entity before every order-sensitive loop (does not address either cause and taxes every system); leaving body plans per frame and accepting that `--speed` changes the run.
**Accepted tradeoff:** headless `--speed 1` no longer paces to the wall clock; every headless speed runs as fast as the CPU allows and speed only sets ticks per frame. Regenerated food is visible to the same tick's snapshot rather than the next tick's, and newborns get their `BodyPlan` at the end of their birth tick rather than at the frame end; both change the run a given seed produces relative to before, not the rules. GUI runs stay wall-clock paced. With no per-frame system touching simulation state any more they may well be reproducible too, but that has not been measured. `SimRng` state is still not saved, so a loaded world diverges from the run that saved it. The CI determinism probe (`docs/QUALITY.md`) now guards this property: a mismatch there is a regression.

### Imported creatures are founders, and the innovation counter jumps past them
**Chosen:** `--seed-with` genomes are appended to the founding population and go through `spawn_initial_population` with everyone else: the per-biome allocation is computed over the enlarged total, every founder (generated or imported) draws its biome slot, tile and offset from `SimRng` in one shuffled sequence, and imports start at the same 90% of their reproduction threshold with `Generation(0)` and `SpeciesId(0)`. Before any founder is generated the `InnovationCounter` is raised to one past the largest innovation number any import carries. A creature file holds the same `SaveGenome` shape as a save, plus optional provenance.
**Alternatives:** spawn imports after the founders with a separate placement loop (a second code path to keep in step with the founder rules, and a second RNG consumer to order); renumber imported connections onto the new world's counter (loses the NEAT meaning of matching innovation numbers between two imports from the same origin world, which is what lets them cross over sensibly); leave the counter alone and accept collisions (a generated founder's connection and an imported one could share a number while joining different neurons, which `compatibility_distance` and crossover would read as the same gene); keep imports at their exported energy and generation (an import with a full energy bar would breed on tick one, which the founder energy rule exists to prevent).
**Why:** one spawn path means an import can never lack a component a founder has, and the placement rule cannot drift between the two. Drawing from `SimRng` keeps a seeded `--seed-with` run reproducible; two runs with the same seed and files are bit-identical, and a run with no imports consumes the RNG exactly as before, so existing seeds are unchanged. The counter jump is the cheapest way to make innovation numbers unique across the mixed population without touching the imported genomes.
**Accepted tradeoff:** with imports present the generated founders' genomes differ from the same seed without them (the slot shuffle covers more slots and the counter starts higher), so `--seed-with` defines a new run rather than "the seed-1 run plus two creatures". A loaded save ignores the flag with a warning: founders only exist at world start, and dropping an organism into a running world would need its own placement and energy rules.

### Spatial hash is rebuilt inside the fixed tick, not once per frame
**Chosen:** `update_spatial_hash` (defined in `clauvolution_world`) is scheduled by `SimPlugin` as the second system in the `FixedUpdate` chain, directly after `tick_counter_system`. `WorldPlugin` does not register it.
**Alternatives:** rebuild in `PreUpdate` once per frame (what shipped originally); rebuild lazily on first query per tick; maintain the hash incrementally as positions change.
**Why:** positions change once per fixed tick, and a frame can run many fixed ticks — every frame in headless at the default `--speed 10`, and any GUI frame above 1× or after a hitch. With a per-frame rebuild, every tick after the first in a frame queried neighbour cells from the start of the frame, so headless sweeps and the watched sim were not running the same physics, and every tuning number taken from headless before this change was measured with a stale hash. Rebuilding at the head of the tick is the smallest change that makes each tick self-consistent. Lazy and incremental variants would save the rebuild cost on ticks that do not need it, but the rebuild is a linear pass over positions and is not where the tick spends its time.
**Accepted tradeoff:** the hash reflects positions at the start of the tick. Systems after `action_system` (predation, disease, symbiosis, reproduction) query with post-move positions against pre-move cells; because every reader re-checks real distance after the lookup, the effect is that an entity which moved across a cell boundary this tick can be missed, never falsely matched. That one-move lag is bounded by per-tick speed and is accepted. The ordering is enforced by the chain, and a `debug_assert` in `sensing_and_brain_system` (which runs before anything moves) fails a debug build if the hash and positions ever disagree. Since 2026-09-20 the hash indexes organisms only: food has its own `FoodSnapshot` and every reader resolves hits through an organism query, so the food entries (1.3k to 13k against 2000 organisms on seed 42) were walked and rejected for nothing. Measured at `--headless 3000 --seed 42` on a loaded machine, the wall time did not move (about 140 s either way at comparable load), so this is a cleanliness change at today's population, not a measured speed-up; the tick cost lives elsewhere. Measured on 2026-09-23 with per-system CPU timing at `--headless 1000 --seed 42`: nearly half of it was the food-eating loop in `action_system` (46 of 101 CPU seconds, and about 85% of each tick in the opening burst while the world still holds 5k to 13k food items), which walks the whole food snapshot for every eater and rescans the tick's eaten list for every item it passes. The fix, a per-snapshot eaten flag in place of the rescan, landed in #50; after it, sensing was the dominant cost, which the next entry addresses.

### Sensing reads per-tick grids, not the ECS
**Chosen (2026-09-24):** `sensing_and_brain_system` copies what it reads about organisms (position, size, species, photo hint, whether it is a photosynthesiser, signal) into a `CellGrid` once per tick, filled cell by cell from the spatial hash with the hash's keys, and bins the `FoodSnapshot` into a second `CellGrid` on 32-unit cells. Each organism's neighbour scan walks the cells `query_radius` would have returned, in its order (dx outer, dy inner, each cell in the hash's order), skipping only cells whose every point lies farther than the sensing range plus `CELL_REACH_MARGIN` (0.5 world units). The nearest-food scan visits only the food cells that can reach the range, and on equal distances keeps the item earlier in the snapshot, which is what the full scan over the snapshot chose. Brain evaluation moved to slots in the same change; see the next entry.
**Alternatives:** keep `query_radius` and snapshot only the ECS reads (removes the lookups but keeps an allocated `Vec` per organism, which was about half the query cost); make the spatial hash itself store the sensed fields (every other reader of the hash would carry fields it does not use, and grazing and predation are being edited elsewhere); a k-d tree for food (more code for a structure rebuilt every tick; the grid is a counting sort).
**Why:** measured with temporary per-phase CPU timing inside the parallel closure at `--headless 2000 --seed 42`, sensing was about 52 CPU seconds: neighbour lookups through the ECS query 19.2 s, `query_radius` (allocation and copies included) 14.8 s, brain evaluation 12.0 s, the nearest-food scan over the whole snapshot 4.1 s, input assembly 0.6 s and writing outputs (dropping the old activation map included) 1.3 s. The nearest-eater search already ran in the same pass as the nearest-organism search. After the change: grid rebuilds 0.15 s, food scan 1.8 s, neighbour scan 6.9 s, input assembly 0.65 s, brain evaluation 0.9 s, output writes 0.5 s, about 11 s in total. Whole-run instructions retired (`/usr/bin/time -l`) fell from 937G to 507G on seed 42 and from 605G to 336G on seed 3 at 2000 ticks; user CPU from 96.7 s to about 42 s and from 68.3 s to about 30 s, on a shared machine. History CSVs and summaries are byte-identical to the previous code on both seeds (md5 `d0633b30f3923045ae9bb6c9a38b2a7e` and `9d823bb63ca7cc1c04cfdf99e1072cb0`). Food cells of 64 units measured within half a percent of 32.
**Accepted tradeoff:** two more structures to keep in step with the hash. The neighbour scan's tie order depends on visiting cells in `query_radius`'s order, and both use `SpatialHash::cell_range`; a change to the hash's cell order changes sensing's too, which is the property that matters. The margin is what makes skipping cells exact: a cell is skipped only when it is at least half a world unit clear of the range, far beyond rounding in `floor(pos / cell_size)` or the distance. After this change sensing is no longer the largest system: in a `sample` profile of the same run, grazing, symbiosis tracking and disease transmission each cost about as much as sensing, mostly in `query_radius`; see "Neighbour queries after sensing read per-system grids".

### Brains evaluate over slots, not a map
**Chosen (2026-09-24):** `Brain::from_genome` gives every neuron id it mentions a slot and compiles the non-input neurons, in the existing topological order, into steps of (slot, bias, activation, incoming source slots and weights in genome order). `evaluate_into` runs them over a `Vec<f32>` it clears and resizes, which `BrainActivations` keeps between ticks; `Brain::activation` reads one neuron's value back for the heatmap.
**Alternatives:** keep the `HashMap<u64, f32>` and reuse its allocation (removes the allocation and rehash but keeps a SipHash per read and write); pad every brain to a fixed size for SIMD or a GPU (`gpu-brain-compute-shader`; not needed at this population).
**Why:** evaluation built a fresh map on every call, and the old activation map was dropped every tick: 12.0 plus about 1 CPU second of the 52 s sensing cost above. It is now 0.9 s. The arithmetic is unchanged (each sum starts from the bias and adds incoming values in the same order, and a slot never written reads 0.0 as a missing key did), so outputs are bit-identical; `slot_evaluation_matches_the_map_evaluator_bit_for_bit` checks outputs and every neuron's activation against the old evaluator, kept in the test module, over forty mutated genomes, including a connection from a neuron the genome does not list.
**Accepted tradeoff:** `BrainActivations` is only meaningful together with the `Brain` that filled it; reading it through a different brain gives wrong values, and an index past the end reads 0.0.

### Neighbour queries after sensing read per-system grids
**Chosen (2026-09-25):** grazing, predation, disease transmission and symbiosis tracking each keep a `CellGrid` in a `Local`, refilled at the start of the system from the spatial hash through `CellGrid::rebuild_from_hash`, and walk it with `CellGrid::for_each_near`, which visits the cells `query_radius` would have visited in its order (dx outer, dy inner, each cell in the hash's order). Each grid holds only the neighbours its system can act on, with the fields it reads, taken from the ECS at the start of the system: grazing holds living photosynthesisers (position, energy); predation holds living organisms (position, energy, size, armour value times size, whether a plant); disease holds infected organisms only (position, severity, ticks remaining); symbiosis holds every organism's position. Sensing fills its own grid through the same `rebuild_from_hash`. Mate search in `reproduction_system` still calls `query_radius`; it is about 2% of the tick and runs only for parents that want a child.
**Alternatives:** one shared grid built once per tick after `update_spatial_hash` (each system needs different fields and a different filter, readers after `action_system` need post-move positions, and the unit tests that insert into the hash and run one system would each need the extra rebuild step; a rebuild is a linear pass over about 2000 entries, expected to be well under a percent of the tick (not measured separately; sensing's two rebuilds measured 0.15 s of about 11 s)); make `SpatialHash` itself dense (it lives in the world crate, and every other reader would change with it); skip out-of-range cells as sensing does (not exact here: these systems test distance against positions that moved after the hash was built, so an entity can sit outside the cell it is filed under, and symbiosis has no range test at all, taking the nearest organism in any visited cell).
**Why:** each call to `query_radius` looked up 25 to 49 cells through a SipHash `HashMap`, copied the hits into a fresh `Vec` (with its `realloc` and `memmove`), and each hit then went through an ECS `get`. The grid lookup is an index into a dense array, nothing is allocated per query, and the filter at rebuild removes most hits before the walk (disease visits only the few infected organisms). Every value a system read through `get` during its scan is one nothing changes until the scan ends (bites, kills and new infections are applied after it), so the snapshot holds the values the lookups read; filtering at rebuild drops entries that each loop would have skipped anyway, and it does not reorder the rest, so ties, float accumulation order and RNG draws are unchanged. `cell_grid_tests` checks the walk against `query_radius` item for item, with and without a filter.
**Profile before (2026-09-25):** a `sample` profile of `--headless 2000 --seed 42` (release, origin/main at 63a866b), counting each system's inclusive samples across all threads: sensing 25.0%, grazing 24.0%, symbiosis tracking 18.7%, disease transmission 16.1%, predation 6.9%, action 5.1%, everything else under 2% each. The top of stack is `SpatialHash::query_radius`, `memmove` and `realloc` from growing its result `Vec`, SipHash for the per-cell `HashMap` lookups (a query of radius 20 on 16-unit cells looks up 49 cells), and the ECS `get` that resolves each hit. Whole run: 507.8G instructions retired on seed 42 and 336.9G on seed 3.
**After:** inclusive samples in the same profile, before and after: grazing 10,375 to 1,063, symbiosis tracking 8,076 to 2,232, disease transmission 6,960 to 899, predation 2,999 to 656; sensing about 11,000 in both. Sensing is now just over half the tick again, then symbiosis tracking (11%) and action (11%). Instructions retired, whole run at 2000 ticks, after each step on seed 42: 507.8G, grazing 364.0G, disease 292.8G, symbiosis 241.4G, predation 198.3G; seed 3 went from 336.9G to about 133G (132.4G to 133.0G across runs). User CPU on a shared machine: seed 42 from about 44 to 48 s down to about 20 to 24 s, seed 3 from about 30 s to about 14 to 17 s. History CSVs and summaries are byte-identical to origin/main after every step (seed 42 `e2a2d2da2c86b186bd63b990a36a4ace`, seed 3 `27508e08422876a6e102eb8ebda46894`).
**Accepted tradeoff:** four more grids that must be filled from the hash, not from a query over positions, or the walk order changes. A system that starts mutating a neighbour's fields during its scan would have to update or drop its snapshot, or it would read stale values where `get` read fresh ones.

### Death markers expire in the sim, not the renderer
**Chosen (2026-09-23):** `death_marker_expiry_system` in the `SimTick` chain, directly before `death_system`, counts every `DeathMarker` down by one fixed timestep and despawns it at zero (`DEATH_MARKER_SECS`, 0.5 s of virtual time, 15 ticks). The render crate's `update_death_markers` only attaches the sprite and scales it from the timer. Before this, only the render system counted the timer down and despawned markers, so a headless run, which has no render crate, kept one entity per death for the rest of the run.
**Alternatives:** skip spawning markers when the render plugin is absent (adds a mode flag the sim has to consult, and headless and GUI worlds would then hold different entities); keep the render system as the owner and add a headless-only cleanup system (two owners for one lifetime, and the headless one would sit outside the shared tick).
**Why:** one owner in both modes, inside the chain, so the marker's lifetime is part of the tick like every other entity the sim spawns. The ECS census at 5000 ticks: seed 42 went from 71,472 entities (69,000 of them markers) to 2,659 (187 markers); seed 3 from 46,759 (44,139 markers) to 2,785 (165 markers). The spatial hash was already unaffected, since it indexes organisms only (see the entry above): cell count, entry count and largest cell were identical before and after at every 1000-tick sample. Same-seed history CSVs and summaries were byte-identical before and after on seeds 42 and 3 at 5000 ticks; nothing in the tick reads markers, and despawning them only changes which entity indices later spawns reuse, which no system orders by. Wall time did not move measurably on a heavily loaded machine (589.8 s against 544.0 s on seed 42, 379.4 s against 393.1 s on seed 3), so this is a memory and cleanliness fix, not a speed-up.
**Accepted tradeoff:** the GUI fade now advances once per fixed tick instead of once per frame, so on a 60 Hz display at 1x it steps every other frame rather than gliding. The lifetime in real time is unchanged at every speed, because the old per-frame timer also counted virtual time. This has not been checked visually.

### Rayon parallelism via Bevy `par_iter_mut`, not rayon crate directly
**Chosen:** Used Bevy's `Query::par_iter_mut` in `sensing_and_brain_system`. Bevy's task pool wraps Rayon under the hood.
**Alternatives considered:** direct rayon (pull in as dep), manual collect → `par_iter` → apply, custom thread pool.
**Why:** Bevy's idiomatic parallel iteration is already set up, safe with Bevy's ECS borrow checker, and requires no extra deps or custom thread pools. Writes to per-organism components are conflict-free because each iteration gets its own `Mut<T>`.
**Accepted tradeoff:** we don't control the pool size; Bevy picks based on cores. Measured ~1.4 effective cores used in practice — less than the 6 available because brain eval isn't the sole per-tick bottleneck. Spatial queries in sensing and the rest of the tick (metabolism, reproduction, disease) also contribute. Further parallelisation of those would pay off; wasn't in scope for v1.

### Screenshotting egui overlays: Bevy + bevy_egui can't do it out of the box
**Context:** we wanted `S` (manual) and the `--script` tour to produce images that include the header bar, side panel, and minimap legend. With Bevy 0.15.3 and bevy_egui 0.33.0, out-of-the-box `Screenshot::primary_window()` captures the main camera's render but **not** the egui overlay on top of it. The panel appears to "vanish" in the saved image.

**Why this happens (the short version):**
- bevy_egui draws directly to `window.swap_chain_texture_view` instead of going through Bevy's `ViewTarget` abstraction. See `bevy_egui-0.33.0/src/egui_node.rs` line ~285 — the render target is hardcoded to the swap chain.
- Bevy's `Screenshot` API works by replacing the window target's `OutputColorAttachment` with a capture texture, so anything rendered via `ViewTarget` (main camera) lands in the capture. Egui, rendering straight to the swap chain, completely bypasses this redirection.
- We'd happily read back from the swap chain after egui draws, except Bevy configures the surface with `TextureUsages::RENDER_ATTACHMENT` only (see `bevy_render-0.15.3/src/view/window/mod.rs` line ~356). Without `COPY_SRC` on the swap chain texture, wgpu refuses the readback.

**Why this feels like a Bevy/bevy_egui failure:** three small integration decisions — egui bypassing `ViewTarget`, screenshot going through `ViewTarget`, swap chain not being `COPY_SRC` — combine to make "capture a frame of the app as the user sees it" impossible with the stock APIs. Each decision is individually defensible; together they box us out. Fixing any one of them upstream would resolve this.

**Chosen:** render the frame a second time into an `Image` we own. `begin_screenshot` (in `clauvolution_render::screenshot_with_egui`) creates an image with `RENDER_ATTACHMENT | COPY_SRC | TEXTURE_BINDING`, spawns a secondary `Camera2d` targeting it with the main camera's transform and projection cloned, and attaches bevy_egui's `EguiRenderToImage` to the primary window entity so the same egui context is also drawn onto that image. bevy_egui's default graph edge draws egui *before* the camera (its use case is "egui as a texture in the scene"), which would let the camera clear over the UI, so a small `ExtractSchedule` system flips the edge so egui composites after the camera. A few frames later a `Readback::texture` observer receives the pixels, converts BGRA to RGBA, strips wgpu's row padding, writes the PNG, and despawns the secondary camera. One capture is in flight at a time; the tour script waits on it.

**Alternatives considered:**
- Patch Bevy's surface configuration to add `COPY_SRC` to the swap chain and copy it out after `egui_pass`. Was the first plan; rejected because it modifies Bevy's window setup and depends on the platform surface accepting `COPY_SRC`.
- `screencapture` CLI shell-out on macOS. Shipped briefly, reverted — platform-specific, captures the entire monitor, required `osascript` to activate our window first.
- Fork bevy_egui to render via `ViewTarget`. Ongoing upstream maintenance burden for a personal project.
- Render everything (camera + egui) to an intermediate texture we own, then blit to the swap chain for display. Needs bevy_egui cooperation we don't have.
- Accept that screenshots miss egui. Would have been fine if we didn't want UI overlays in README images.

**Accepted tradeoff:** the world is rendered twice for the capture frame, and the graph-edge flip is coupled to bevy_egui's internal node labels and its `setup_new_render_to_image_nodes_system`, so a bevy_egui upgrade may need the patch refitted. The legacy `--screenshot` tour still uses Bevy's stock `Screenshot::primary_window()` and therefore produces camera-only images; `--script` is the egui-aware path.

### Incremental release builds
**Chosen:** `[profile.release] incremental = true` in Cargo.toml.
**Alternatives:** default non-incremental release (much slower rebuilds).
**Why:** during active development, rebuild times matter. After first build, single-file changes rebuild in seconds rather than minutes.
**Accepted tradeoff:** slightly larger release artifacts, potentially slightly worse runtime perf. For this project we don't care — there's no "distribution" binary.

### 10 workspace crates, one per domain
**Chosen:** sim/world/genome/brain/body/phylogeny/render/ui/core/app as separate crates.
**Alternatives:** one big crate with modules, three crates (sim / render / app), two crates (headless + app).
**Why:** enforces that sim crates can't import from render or ui. Compile parallelism when only one crate changes. Clear ownership — a bug in rendering lives in one place, a bug in genetics lives in another.
**Accepted tradeoff:** extra Cargo.toml files, slight compile-time overhead for workspace resolution, component/resource types have to live in `core` to be shared.

### Save format: every genome field has a default
**Chosen (2026-09-21):** `SaveGenome` in `clauvolution_sim::save` carries a container-level `#[serde(default)]` backed by a hand-written `Default` impl, so a save file that predates a field loads with that field at a neutral value instead of failing to parse. The rule for a new genome field: add it to `SaveGenome` and to its `Default` impl, and pick the value there. Zero when zero reads as the trait being absent (no armour, no claws, a land-dweller, a neutral symbiont, a generalist diet); the midpoint of the founder range in `Genome::new_minimal_with_diet` when zero is outside the trait's `*_BOUNDS` or describes a degenerate body (`body_size` 1.0, `speed_factor` 1.0, `sense_range` 55.0). An empty `neurons` or `body_segments` list is not a usable genome, and `validate_save_state` drops that organism with a warning rather than aborting the load.
**Alternatives:** per-field `#[serde(default)]` on each trait as it is added (the previous practice, applied to three of ten traits and forgotten for the rest); a versioned save format with explicit migrations; refusing old saves outright.
**Why:** a save is the only way to carry a world across a code change, and the project adds traits often. Before this, a pre-`sense_range` save failed on the first organism and the whole file was lost. The container-level attribute plus a struct-literal `Default` means the compiler rejects a new field until someone has chosen its default, which is the part that was being skipped. A versioned format with migrations is the right answer once fields change meaning or type rather than merely appear; nothing has yet.
**Accepted tradeoff:** a field removed from the struct is silently ignored on load, and a field whose meaning changes gets the old value with no warning. The three founder-midpoint defaults are constants copied from the founder ranges rather than derived from them; if the founder ranges move, the defaults need a matching edit. `SaveState` and `SaveOrganism` follow a variant of this rule with some fields still required; see the next entry.

### Save format: every field has a default unless the world cannot be rebuilt without it
**Chosen (2026-09-23):** `SaveState` and `SaveOrganism` put a per-field `#[serde(default)]` on every field with a neutral value and leave the rest required, so a save missing a required field is still rejected with serde's "missing field" message. Required on `SaveState`: `tick`, because every phylogeny, chronicle and extinction tick in the file is relative to it; `terrain_seed`, because the terrain is regenerated from it and any guessed seed puts the population on a different map, with aquatic organisms stranded and plants on rock, while nothing in the load says so; `organisms`, because without them there is no world to resume. Defaulted on `SaveState`: `season_tick` 0 (start of the cycle), `stats` all zero (display counters, and `SaveStats` itself takes a container-level default), `food` empty (regeneration refills it from the first tick), `phylo_nodes` empty (every lookup by species id already tolerates a missing node), `chronicle_entries` empty, and `innovation_counter` 0. Required on `SaveOrganism`: `x`, `y`, `energy` and `genome`, since an organism has no neutral position, its energy is the ledger's starting balance, and there is nothing to spawn without a genome. Defaulted on `SaveOrganism`: the values a founder spawns with, which are `health` 1.0, `age` 0, `generation` 0, `species_id` 0 (unclassified, reassigned on the next classification pass), `signal` 0.0 and `memory` zeroed. A defaulted innovation counter would hand out numbers the loaded genomes already carry and make unrelated connections look homologous, so `validate_save_state` now raises the counter above the highest innovation in the loaded genomes, with a warning. A save written by `save_world` already satisfies that and is unchanged.
**Revised (2026-09-24): the rule now covers `SavePhyloNode` and `SaveFood` too.** Both first kept every field required, on the argument that `SavePhyloNode::name` has no neutral value and a blank species name reads as a display bug. But `name` was added after the first save format, so every save written before species naming was refused whole, which contradicts "older saves load". A placeholder name is a better outcome than no world. Required on `SavePhyloNode`: `species_id`, the key organisms, chronicle entries and child nodes refer to. Defaulted: `parent_id` none (a root species), `born_tick` 0, `extinct_tick` none (living; the next population update marks it extinct if it has no members), `peak_population` 0 (raised on the next update), `strategy` omnivore (the code every unknown value already maps to), and `name` empty, which `restore_phylo` replaces with `placeholder_species_name`, "Species N", the name the tree already gives a species recorded without traits. The original name cannot be recomputed: the generator works from the species' traits, which the node does not store, and an extinct species has no members to derive them from. The placeholder is two words, so it never matches a generated three-word name, and a child of such a species gets a fresh generated name. Required on `SaveFood`: `x` and `y`, since a food item has no neutral position. Defaulted: `energy`, to `SimConfig::food_energy_value`, what regeneration gives new food; the field is an `Option` so `spawn_saved_food` can fill it from the config, and a saved value is written as before.
**Alternatives:** a container-level default on both structs, as `SaveGenome` has. That would make a new field a compile error until someone picks its default, but it cannot leave `terrain_seed` required: serde fills a missing field from the struct's `Default` whatever it is, so the choice would be a random seed, a fixed seed, or an `Option` that `load_world` then rejects by hand. A random or fixed seed loads a different world while appearing to load the saved one. The `Option` works but spreads the rejection across two places for the benefit of one field. Defaulting `terrain_seed` with a warning was also considered and rejected, because the warning is easy to miss and the result is not the saved world. The gene records (`SaveNeuron`, `SaveConnection`, `SaveBodySegment`) keep every field required, since each has been present since the first save format and a partly described gene cannot be rebuilt.
**Why:** the fields most likely to be added later are the ones with a natural starting value, such as new per-organism brain or physiology state, and those should not cost the user the whole file. The fields left required are the ones whose absence means the file cannot describe the world that was saved, and a clear refusal is better than a plausible-looking wrong world.
**Accepted tradeoff:** unlike `SaveGenome`, the compiler does not force the choice. A new `SaveState` or `SaveOrganism` field without `#[serde(default)]` is required, which fails safe (an older file is refused, never silently wrong) but repeats the "forgot the attribute" failure that the genome rule was written to stop. The tests in `save.rs` list the required fields and remove each defaulted one, so a reviewer sees the choice in the diff. Saving terrain state (next entry) did not change `terrain_seed`: terrain type, elevation and light still come only from the seed, so it stays required.

### Save format: terrain persists only the tile fields that change
**Chosen (2026-09-23):** `SaveState::terrain` holds a `SaveTerrain` record with the map's width and height and one value per tile for the four fields that change after `TileMap::generate`: `temperature` (the ice age), `moisture` (the ice age, niche construction), `nutrients` (the volcano, niche construction) and `vegetation_density` (`tile_dynamics_system` growth every tick, niche construction). Each field is stored as base64 of its little-endian `f32` bytes, so a load restores the exact bits. On load the terrain is regenerated from `terrain_seed` as before, and the record then overwrites those four fields. `terrain`, `elevation` and `light_level` are never written at runtime and are not stored. The field is `#[serde(default)]`, per the rule in the previous entry: a save without it, or one whose record has the wrong number of values or a non-finite value, or one saved at a different world size, loads with the regenerated terrain and a warning printed to stderr and logged in the chronicle.
**Alternatives:** the full tilemap, which also stores the three fields the seed already determines and would repeat the terrain type as seven fields per tile (about 15.9 MB in decimal JSON for the 512x512 world, against 5.6 MB for this record). Decimal JSON arrays for the four fields (about 9.1 MB; readable but not smaller, and the round trip depends on the float formatter). Storing only tiles that differ from the regenerated map, which saves nothing because vegetation growth changes almost every land tile within a few ticks. Quantising to `u16` (about half the size, but not exact, and every load would drift slightly from the saved world). Deflating the bytes (about 2.9 MB after base64, via `flate2`, which Bevy already pulls in): a real saving, but an opaque blob and one more step to debug in a hand-inspected file; worth reconsidering if save size becomes a problem.
**Why:** the loaded world should be the saved world. Before this, every load quietly reset niche construction and vegetation growth to tick 0, and the ice age and volcano effects disappeared. Storing only what changes keeps the seed as the single source for the map's shape, and adds no second copy of the terrain type that could disagree with it.
**Accepted tradeoff:** measured on a `--headless 300 --seed 42` save, the file grows from 8.5 MB to 14.1 MB. Loading, running one tick and saving again takes about 0.5 s with or without the terrain field, so load time is unaffected. The record is only correct on top of the terrain the same seed and code generate: if `TileMap::generate` changes, an older save gets the new terrain types with the old fields laid over them, as happened for all saves before this entry (see "Terrain noise ranges: elevation -1..1, moisture 0..1" above). A new tile field that changes at runtime has to be added to `SaveTerrain` by hand. `SaveTerrain::from_tile_map` destructures `Tile` with no `..`, so a new field does not compile until someone classifies it as stored or regenerated; the compiler cannot tell whether that classification is right. `BloomEffects` and other temporary event state are not saved.

### Brain inputs grow by migration
**Chosen (2026-09-23, with the nearest-eater inputs):** a genome loaded from a save or a creature file (`--seed-with`) with fewer input neurons than `NUM_INPUTS` is migrated in `save_to_genome` by `Genome::migrate_input_layout`. Input neurons carry ids `0..NUM_INPUTS` and outputs the next `NUM_OUTPUTS`, so an older genome's outputs sit where the new inputs go. Every neuron id at or above the old input count (outputs and hidden neurons) moves up by the number of new inputs, connections are rewritten to match, and the new inputs are added with no connections. Innovation numbers are unchanged. The brain reads inputs and outputs by sorted id, which the shift preserves, and an unconnected input contributes nothing, so a migrated brain gives exactly the outputs it gave before for the old inputs; `migrated_legacy_brain_gives_the_same_outputs_for_the_old_inputs` (brain crate) checks this over twenty mutated 22-input genomes with hidden neurons, and `genome_with_the_old_input_count_migrates_on_load` (save module) checks both load paths. A genome whose inputs are not exactly `0..k` is left as written. `load_world` logs how many organisms it migrated.
**Alternatives:** leave old genomes as written (they would still run, since the brain finds inputs and outputs by neuron type, but the lineage could never wire the new inputs, and old and current genomes in one world, such as an imported creature among founders, would use ids 22 to 25 for an output in one and an input in the other, which nothing in crossover checks); refuse old genomes (loses every save and export for a change with an exact mechanical rewrite); give new inputs ids above every existing one (no remap needed, but new and migrated genomes would then place the same input at different ids, and crossover matches neurons by id); a versioned save format (the save-format entry above keeps that for fields that change meaning; this is a layout change with an exact mechanical rewrite, detected from the genome itself).
**Why:** a save and an exported creature are the only ways to carry a lineage across a code change, and the migration is one function with an exact behavioural guarantee.
**Accepted tradeoff:** hidden neuron ids in a migrated genome differ from those in the file. Hidden ids are per-genome (`max + 1` at the split), so the shift applies to every genome in a loaded world alike and matching between them is kept. The rule assumes inputs are appended; a future change that inserts an input in the middle, or adds outputs, needs its own migration step.
