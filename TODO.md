- [BRAIN] `crossover-topology-from-initiator` — **Sexual reproduction never combines brain structure from two parents.** `Genome::crossover` gives the child exactly `self`'s connection set (the other parent's disjoint and excess genes are dropped), and `reproduction_system` makes `self` whichever parent it visits first in query order, not a fitter one, so two lineages' structural innovations can never meet in one child.
  - Evidence: in `crossover`, the merge loop's `else { j += 1 }` skips `other`'s lower-numbered unmatched genes and only `self`'s tail is appended after the loop, so `other` contributes only the weight and enabled flag of shared genes, 30% of the time some body segments, and half of each scalar trait. The graft that pulls `other`'s neurons in by id can only fire when a matching gene names a neuron `self` lacks, which shared descent never produces under the per-connection counter. In `reproduction_system` the initiator is the first of the pair in `Query` iteration order (archetype, then table row; gaining or losing an `Infection` component moves an organism between archetypes, and despawns reorder rows by swap-remove), it alone passes the ceiling admission draw and must clear the size-scaled threshold, and it alone pays. Nothing reads energy to choose it. The mate is marked as mated and cannot initiate that tick, but keeps its energy and can pass on its own topology on a later tick, so its innovations are delayed rather than suppressed.
  - Starting point: Decide whether this matters to what is watched. Options are classic NEAT's equal-fitness rule (take each disjoint and excess gene from either parent at random), a fitter-parent rule with energy at mating as the proxy, or recording the current "the paying parent passes its structure" rule in DECISIONS. Any change alters mating dynamics and needs a tuning pass. It interacts with `plans/2026-09-24-innovation-keying.md`, which makes unrelated genomes share genes and so gives crossover more matching genes to mix, and with the review finding `mate-energy-unscaled-and-unpaid`, which covers the mate's threshold and cost.
  - Source: fix/crossover-parent-doc branch, 2026-09-24
  - Related: `species-distance-unrelated-ceiling`

- [BRAIN] `species-distance-unrelated-ceiling` — **Unrelated brains score at most 1.0 apart, so a drifting organism almost always fits some other species.** Every connection gets a fresh innovation number, so two genomes of separate descent share no genes, and the NEAT terms give them `0.5 × (a + b) / max(a, b)`: exactly 1.0 for equal connection counts and less for unequal ones, against a join threshold of 1.0. On seeds 42, 3 and 7 at 5000 ticks, 93 to 100% of organisms sat within 1.0 of another species' representative on every pass (99 to 100% on average), and of the organisms found past 1.3 from their own representative (657, 1481 and 485 across the passes) only 0, 58 and 1 were more than 1.0 from every other one, so a new species needs the rare member that is past both at once. Seed 42 still forms only 3 species after its founders with the stay-threshold fix.
  - Starting point: Normalising the structural terms by `a + b` instead of `max(a, b)` makes unrelated genomes score 1.0 plus the body term whatever their sizes; tried on 2026-09-23 it founded 353 to 355 species from the 400 founders at tick 151 on seeds 42, 3 and 7, and the runs were several times slower, so it needs the threshold sweep repeated and probably a founding population that shares innovation numbers, or innovation numbers keyed by (from, to) as in NEAT proper. The measurements and the discarded representative variants are in the "Species classification" DECISIONS entry.

Re-keying the 5000-tick saves of seeds 42, 3 and 7 by `(from, to)` left the share of organisms within 1.0 of another species' representative at 99.5, 99.6 and 100.0% (99.0, 99.6, 100.0 with legacy numbers) and the isolated drifters at 2, 1 and 0 (unchanged).
